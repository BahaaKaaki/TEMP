# Benefits tracking and realization

## When to use this skill
Use when a transformation carries explicit value commitments such as savings, revenue uplift, productivity, service improvement, or risk reduction and leadership needs visibility on realization. Typical asks include VRO design, benefits dashboards, value tracking packs, and realization governance. Do not use for a simple KPI dashboard or a one-off business case with no ongoing realization requirement.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as value logic, baselines and targets, owner accountability, current status, and corrective actions.

## Typical output
Typical length: 8–12 pages in a standard version. This is the default structure for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for value realization  
**What it includes:** benefit logic, current status, gaps, ownership, actions  
**Typical formats:** message page; value summary; realization dashboard

### 1) Benefits logic and taxonomy — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What value categories are being tracked and why  
**What it includes:** benefit tree, categories, definitions, scope of value  
**Typical formats:** benefits tree; logic page; taxonomy table

### 2) Baselines, targets, and assumptions — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What the program is expected to deliver and from where  
**What it includes:** baseline values, targets, phasing, assumptions, gross vs net logic  
**Typical formats:** target table; baseline snapshot; value waterfall

### 3) Initiative linkage and owner accountability — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Who is expected to deliver which benefit and through what initiative  
**What it includes:** initiative-to-benefit mapping, owners, timing, dependencies  
**Typical formats:** owner matrix; linkage map; initiative-benefit table

### 4) Realization dashboard and status — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How performance is tracked in period  
**What it includes:** actual vs target, trend, RAG logic, realized vs at risk  
**Typical formats:** dashboard; bridge; RAG summary

### 5) Variance analysis and corrective actions — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What is driving gaps and what will be done  
**What it includes:** variance drivers, underdelivery causes, corrective actions, decision points  
**Typical formats:** variance bridge; issue heatmap; action tracker

### 6) Governance and review cadence — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How value realization is governed  
**What it includes:** review forums, frequency, escalation, sign-off rules, reporting owners  
**Typical formats:** governance map; review calendar; escalation flow

## How to build
- define the benefits taxonomy and value logic
- baseline each benefit and make target timing explicit
- link benefits to initiatives, owners, and measurement rules
- build the tracking dashboard and review rhythm
- surface realization risks, variances, and corrective actions
- embed the process in governance rather than treating it as reporting only

## Success criteria
Every benefit has a baseline, target, owner, measurement rule, and timing, and leadership can see where value is landing or slipping.

## Common failure modes
Gross and net value are confused; benefits are double counted; no baseline exists; ownership is vague; dashboard shows status but no corrective action.
