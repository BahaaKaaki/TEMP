# Board / final readout pack

## When to use this skill
Use when leadership needs a board-ready update, approval package, or final close-out readout that lands a decision clearly and economically. Typical asks include board packs, final Steering Committee packs, final client readouts, approval memos translated into deck form, and executive close-out materials. Do not use for a working-team status deck or a deep analytical appendix.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as the context and ask, topline outcomes, key decisions, risks, and next steps.

## Typical output
Typical length: 8–12 pages in a standard version. This is the default structure for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary and key asks — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker and decision page for leadership  
**What it includes:** context, ask, outcome, risk, next step  
**Typical formats:** message page; executive summary; key-asks page

### 1) Context and why now — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Why leadership is seeing the pack at this point  
**What it includes:** decision context, trigger, relevance, what changed since last update  
**Typical formats:** context page; timeline snapshot; message page

### 2) What was delivered / current position — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What has been achieved or where the program stands now  
**What it includes:** delivered outputs, current status, achievement against plan, headline evidence  
**Typical formats:** status dashboard; delivered-vs-plan page; milestone bridge

### 3) Decisions or approvals needed — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What leadership must approve, endorse, or note  
**What it includes:** decision items, options if needed, recommendation, implications  
**Typical formats:** decision table; options matrix; recommendation page

### 4) Benefits, risks, and implications — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What the recommendation means in value and risk terms  
**What it includes:** benefits, residual risks, unresolved issues, stakeholder implications  
**Typical formats:** benefits bridge; risk heatmap; implication page

### 5) Next-step plan and commitments — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What happens after the meeting or sign-off  
**What it includes:** immediate actions, owners, dates, governance implications, closure plan  
**Typical formats:** next-step calendar; action tracker; roadmap

### 6) Backup logic and appendix architecture — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the supporting material is organized without overwhelming the core pack  
**What it includes:** appendix logic, evidence references, optional deep dives, pack hygiene  
**Typical formats:** appendix map; evidence tree; backup menu

## How to build
- define the purpose of the pack and the exact ask being made
- organize the storyline around decisions, not activity history
- summarize outcomes versus plan and isolate what matters now
- show implications, residual risks, and options where relevant
- make the approvals or close-out asks explicit
- support the core pack with only the backup needed to defend it

## Success criteria
The ask is obvious, the evidence is sufficient, the pack is concise, and leadership can make or ratify the decision without hunting for the point.

## Common failure modes
Activity-heavy narrative; unclear ask; too much detail in the core pack; no decision logic; risks and implications buried; no backup discipline.
