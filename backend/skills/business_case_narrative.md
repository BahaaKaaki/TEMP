# Business Case Narrative

## When to use this skill

Use this skill when the work must move beyond ambition and show an investable, executable case: new capability builds, new entities, cluster strategies, platform launches, major capital requests, and board or investment committee asks. The canonical storyline is **Vision & Strategy → Capability Plan → Financials**, then operationalized with risks, implementation roadmap, and KPIs.

Do not use it for a pure strategic perspective or a light discussion paper. This is the skill for “show me the case, the build, and the economics.”

## Narrative shape

### 1) Recommendation / investment thesis

**Purpose**  
State what should be funded, built, or approved and why.

**Typical length**  
1 slide or opening section.

**What must be present**  
The ask; the rationale; and the scale or strategic importance.

**Common failure modes**  
Opening with context instead of the ask; or describing the opportunity without stating what decision is sought.

### 2) Vision & Strategy

**Purpose**  
Show the target state, value proposition, demand logic, and strategic position.

**Typical length**  
3–8 slides.

**What must be present**  
Market need or demand logic; strategic role; where to play; how to win; and a clear articulation of the end state.

**Common failure modes**  
Confusing ambition with strategy; failing to show why this play is attractive; or not clarifying the scope of the proposition.

### 3) Capability Plan

**Purpose**  
Show what must exist operationally to make the strategy real.

**Typical length**  
4–10 slides.

**What must be present**  
A build plan across the key enablers. The internal template uses six: people, process, systems/data, infrastructure, supply chain/operations, and machinery/technology/tools. Governance, risk, and execution timing sit here too.

**Common failure modes**  
Jumping from strategy to financials with no operating bridge; under-specifying capability build; or presenting a laundry list rather than a sequenced plan.

### 4) Financials

**Purpose**  
Translate the case into economics and funding.

**Typical length**  
3–8 slides.

**What must be present**  
Revenue logic; funding need; OpEx/CapEx; return logic or value creation; sensitivities; and contribution to broader strategic or portfolio objectives.

**Common failure modes**  
Financial outputs without assumptions; no bridge from capability build to cost base; or a return case that ignores phasing and ramp-up.

### 5) Risks, roadmap, and KPIs

**Purpose**  
Show how the case will be managed after approval.

**Typical length**  
2–4 slides.

**What must be present**  
Risk register or major risks; an implementation roadmap; and operational / financial KPIs.

**Common failure modes**  
Treating the business case as a one-time approval document; or omitting the measures that would let management know the case is on or off track.

## Page patterns and exhibit types

- **Investment thesis page** — answer-first view of the case.
- **Value logic page** — market need, strategic role, and differentiated proposition.
- **Capability heat map / phased build page** — what must be built, by when, and with what dependencies.
- **Economics page** — funding, economics, sensitivities, and impact.
- **Risk and KPI page** — top risks, mitigation, and success measures.

## House conventions

- Keep the ask explicit.
- Show the bridge from strategy to capability to economics.
- Use phased build logic; major cases rarely materialize all at once.
- Make assumptions visible where they drive economics or timing.
- Tie KPIs to both delivery and value realization.

## Inputs the skill needs

**Required**

- The decision to be made.
- Strategic rationale and demand logic.
- Capability requirements.
- High-level economics.

**Optional**

- Detailed model outputs.
- Scenario cases.
- Benchmark economics.
- Risk appetite and funding constraints.

## Judgment anchors

- The stronger the strategic ambition, the more concrete the capability bridge must be.
- Keep the number of capability enablers stable across the document.
- If economics are still directional, say so and anchor them with named assumptions.
- Put risks in the main story if they materially affect the approval decision.

## Common failure modes

- Strategy without build requirements.
- Capability plan without economics.
- Financials detached from operational reality.
- No phased roadmap.
- No explicit approval ask.

## Done-state checklist

- The approval ask is unmistakable.
- The target state and value proposition are clear.
- The capability build is credible and sequenced.
- The economics can be traced back to strategy and build assumptions.
- Risks and KPIs are visible enough for post-approval management.

## Composition notes

Often composes with Options-to-Recommendation when the business case sits beneath a strategic choice.

## Metadata

- Primary use: investment and capability-build cases
- Typical consumer seniority: manager to partner / investment committee
- Typical output length: 8–20 slides
- Durability: high
- Suggested owner discipline: strategy / corporate finance / transformation
