# Change management and adoption

## When to use this skill
Use when a transformation materially changes roles, behaviors, processes, tools, or customer experience and adoption cannot be assumed. Typical asks include change strategy, adoption plans, stakeholder communications, champion networks, and training-led enablement. Do not use for a pure communications plan with no real behavior or operating change behind it.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as the case for change, stakeholder segments, adoption journey, key interventions, and how adoption will be measured.

## Typical output
Typical length: 8–14 pages in a standard version. This is the default structure for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the change program  
**What it includes:** case for change, affected groups, adoption path, interventions, metrics  
**Typical formats:** message page; change summary; adoption page

### 1) Case for change and adoption objective — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Why the change is happening and what adoption means in practice  
**What it includes:** change rationale, target behaviors, adoption objective, business implications  
**Typical formats:** case-for-change page; adoption objective page; message page

### 2) Stakeholder segmentation and impact — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Who is affected and how  
**What it includes:** stakeholder groups, impact levels, resistance points, support needs  
**Typical formats:** stakeholder map; impact heatmap; segment table

### 3) Adoption journey and barriers — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How stakeholders move from awareness to adoption  
**What it includes:** journey stages, typical concerns, adoption blockers, targeted responses  
**Typical formats:** journey map; barrier heatmap; stage-based page

### 4) Messaging and channel plan — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the story will be told across audiences  
**What it includes:** core narrative, tailored messages, channels, cadence, leadership moments  
**Typical formats:** message house; channel matrix; communication cascade

### 5) Capability building and champions — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How people are equipped to adopt the change  
**What it includes:** training waves, champion network, manager enablement, support mechanisms  
**Typical formats:** training wave plan; champion network; enablement map

### 6) Adoption tracking and feedback — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How change progress is measured  
**What it includes:** adoption KPIs, pulse checks, feedback loops, sentiment and usage monitoring  
**Typical formats:** dashboard; sentiment heatmap; feedback loop

### 7) Phased rollout and risks — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the change lands over time and what could derail it  
**What it includes:** rollout phases, dependencies, high-risk groups, mitigations  
**Typical formats:** rollout roadmap; risk heatmap; milestone plan

## How to build
- define what is changing and who is materially affected
- map stakeholder impacts and adoption barriers
- build the change story and targeted messages
- design the channel mix, training, and champion model
- set adoption metrics and feedback loops
- phase the rollout to support awareness through adoption

## Success criteria
The change is explained clearly, interventions are audience-specific, leaders reinforce the message, and adoption is tracked rather than assumed.

## Common failure modes
Generic communications; no stakeholder segmentation; training detached from actual changes; leaders not visible; no adoption measures or feedback loop.
