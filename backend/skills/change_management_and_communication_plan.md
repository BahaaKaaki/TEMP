# Change Management and Communication Plan

## When to use this skill
Use this skill when the work will change how people operate, not just what the organization intends to do. It is the right skill for operating model changes, new product or platform adoption, functional redesign, policy rollout, capability-building programs, and institutional transformations where the outcome depends on awareness, buy-in, capability build, and sustained new behaviors.

Treat this as a distinct shape from generic stakeholder management. The canonical framework is five-part: segmentation, plan, messaging, channels, and monitoring, varying end-user messaging by readiness stage. Map stakeholder impacts first, then design a detailed plan across Awareness, Willingness, and Ability with tailored interventions. Use this skill when the deliverable needs to answer who is changing, how hard the change lands, what messages and interventions are needed, and how adoption will be tracked.

Do not use it for broad ecosystem stakeholder mapping with limited behavior change. Do not use it for one-off workshop planning. This skill is about adoption over time.

## Deliverable shape

### 1) Change case for action
**Purpose**  
Define why change is required and what must change.

**Typical length**  
1 slide.

**What must be present**  
Clear articulation of the target state, the gap from current state, and the business or institutional consequence of not changing.

**Common failure modes**  
Using generic transformation language; failing to define the concrete shift in roles, behaviors, tools, or processes; or presenting change as abstract aspiration.

### 2) Stakeholder landscape and impact assessment
**Purpose**  
Identify who is affected and how.

**Typical length**  
1–3 slides.

**What must be present**  
Stakeholder groups; nature of change for each group; impact dimensions such as roles, reporting lines, skills, locations, working conditions, performance expectations, or systems; and an impact heatmap.

**Common failure modes**  
Segmenting by org chart only; missing frontline or user-facing audiences; or describing impact without differentiating severity.

### 3) Priority stakeholder groups and readiness journey
**Purpose**  
Prioritize where change effort must be concentrated and what journey each group must move through.

**Typical length**  
1–3 slides.

**What must be present**  
Priority groups; readiness stages such as awareness → understanding → acceptance → ownership or awareness → willingness → ability; and what “good” looks like at each stage.

**Common failure modes**  
Treating all stakeholder groups as equally ready; skipping from awareness to training; or not defining readiness outcomes in observable terms.

### 4) Intervention strategy by audience
**Purpose**  
Design the mix of interventions that will move each priority audience.

**Typical length**  
2–4 slides.

**What must be present**  
Audience-specific intervention mix across leadership engagement, town halls, focus groups, champions, toolkits, training, cutover support, feedback channels, and reinforcement mechanisms.

**Common failure modes**  
Using the same intervention set for everyone; over-relying on email communications; or treating training as the only change lever.

### 5) Communication strategy and message architecture
**Purpose**  
Translate change intent into messages, channels, and cadence.

**Typical length**  
2–4 slides.

**What must be present**  
Target audiences, key messages, messengers, channels, timing, and approval process for communications.

**Common failure modes**  
Messages are generic and centrally written; channels are not matched to audience; no message ownership; or no formal review and sign-off flow.

### 6) Change agent and trainer network
**Purpose**  
Create distributed ownership of the change.

**Typical length**  
1–2 slides.

**What must be present**  
Change champions, trainers, leaders, or super-users; their role; how they are enabled; and how they interact with the central team.

**Common failure modes**  
Naming champions without a job; no enablement plan; or no line of sight between local champions and central messaging.

### 7) Monitoring, feedback, and adaptation
**Purpose**  
Measure whether the change is landing and adjust accordingly.

**Typical length**  
1–2 slides.

**What must be present**  
Feedback mechanisms such as surveys, interviews, focus groups, digital analytics, adoption metrics, and review rhythm; plus rules for updating the plan.

**Common failure modes**  
Monitoring only communications outputs such as open rates; no qualitative feedback loop; or no trigger for course correction.

### 8) Activation roadmap and ownership
**Purpose**  
Show how the plan unfolds over time and who owns each intervention.

**Typical length**  
1–2 slides.

**What must be present**  
Phase-based timeline, intervention sequencing, owners, dependencies, and handover or institutionalization path.

**Common failure modes**  
No link to the broader transformation timeline; unowned actions; or a plan that stops at launch and ignores reinforcement.

## Page patterns and exhibit types
Use a compact set of exhibits.

- **Change impact heatmap** — audiences vs change dimensions.
- **Readiness journey page** — stages and target outcomes.
- **Intervention matrix** — audience, objective, intervention, owner, cadence.
- **Communications cascade** — central team to leaders to champions to end users.
- **Message-channel architecture** — what is said, by whom, how, and when.
- **Monitoring dashboard** — adoption metrics, feedback, and qualitative signals.
- **Phased activation calendar** — awareness, willingness, ability, reinforcement.

## House conventions
- Start with the behavior or operating shift, not with the communications calendar.
- Segment by impact and readiness, not just by hierarchy.
- Design interventions by audience and journey stage.
- Use multiple messengers: leadership, influencers, change champions, functional owners.
- Keep the communications process controlled; draft, review, approve, deliver, and measure.
- Separate awareness from willingness and willingness from ability.
- Pair communications with training, forums, and support mechanisms.
- Monitor both leading indicators and lived-experience feedback.

## Inputs the skill needs
**Required**
- Target state and nature of the change.
- Stakeholder groups affected.
- Impact dimensions for each group.
- Program phases and key milestones.
- Existing communications or change initiatives already running.

**Optional**
- Baseline adoption or sentiment data.
- Current comms templates and brand rules.
- Training curriculum or learning infrastructure.
- Named sponsors or champions.
- Digital analytics capability.

The skill should degrade gracefully if baseline adoption data is missing; it should not degrade gracefully if the target behavior change is unspecified.

## Judgment anchors
- Distinguish true adoption risks from generic communications needs.
- Prioritize groups by impact severity and change criticality, not by seniority alone.
- Choose the readiness model that best fits the transformation; keep it simple and observable.
- Match interventions to the real barrier: awareness, motivation, capability, role clarity, or reinforcement.
- Keep the number of messages small and repeat them consistently across channels.
- Use change champions where local translation matters; do not create them as symbolic roles only.
- Treat monitoring as a management mechanism, not a reporting appendix.

## Common failure modes
- Communications-led plan with no change logic.
- Impact assessment is superficial.
- Training arrives too late or too early relative to process changes.
- Messages are inconsistent across sponsors and channels.
- Feedback is collected but not used to adapt the plan.
- Reinforcement is missing, so adoption decays after launch.

## Done-state checklist
- The target behavior or operating shift is clear.
- Stakeholder impacts are differentiated and prioritized.
- Readiness stages are visible and practical.
- The intervention mix is audience-specific.
- Messages, channels, and owners are defined.
- Champions and trainers have explicit roles.
- Monitoring goes beyond vanity metrics.
- The plan spans launch and reinforcement, not just announcement.

## Composition notes
This skill typically composes with:
- **Stakeholder Engagement Plan**
- **Stakeholder Workshop and Socialization Plan**
- **Governance Cadence and Decision Forum Design**

When change effort is the main execution risk, this skill should lead and the broader stakeholder plan should support it.

## Metadata
- Primary use: transformation, adoption, operating model activation, product/platform rollout
- Typical consumer seniority: manager to director
- Typical output length: 6–15 slides
- Durability: high
- Suggested owner discipline: change management / transformation office
