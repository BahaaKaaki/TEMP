# Commercial due diligence / market attractiveness

## When to use this skill
Use for buy-side or growth-oriented assessments where the question is effectively go / no-go, upside / downside, or which market or asset is most attractive. Do not use for a broad strategy refresh without a transaction or investment question.

## Executive summary
One page only. It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 messages around attractiveness, competitive position, upside, risks, and implication for the deal or decision.

## Typical output
Typical length: 8–14 pages in a standard version. This is the default structure for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page investment or decision tracker  
**What it includes:** answer / recommendation, 3–5 messages, key implication  
**Typical formats:** message page, investment thesis, summary strip

### 1) Investment question and scope — 1 page
**What it is:** What is being tested and why  
**What it includes:** decision question, scope, market / target boundary, key hypotheses  
**Typical formats:** scope page, issue tree, diligence scope

### 2) Market attractiveness — 2–3 pages
**What it is:** Whether the market is attractive enough  
**What it includes:** size, growth, structure, drivers, margins, barriers  
**Typical formats:** market sizing bridge, growth chart, attractiveness matrix

### 3) Competitive landscape and target position — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** How the target or option sits in the market  
**What it includes:** competitors, shares, positioning, differentiation, capabilities  
**Typical formats:** competitor map, peer comparison, benchmark table

### 4) Revenue quality and upside — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Whether growth and economics are credible  
**What it includes:** revenue mix, growth logic, expansion levers, upside sources  
**Typical formats:** revenue mix, waterfall, upside tree, value bridge

### 5) Key risks and deal implications — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What could break the case  
**What it includes:** risks, sensitivities, red flags, key assumptions  
**Typical formats:** risk matrix, sensitivity table, issue list

### 6) Conclusion and recommendation — 1 page
**What it is:** Final view on attractiveness  
**What it includes:** go / no-go, conditions, priorities, next-step implications  
**Typical formats:** recommendation page, decision matrix

## How to build
- define the exact investment or decision question
- size and segment the market
- assess structure, growth, and competition
- test the target’s position and revenue logic
- identify upside sources and key risks
- conclude on attractiveness and decision implications

## Success criteria
- clear answer early
- market and target logic both covered
- upside and downside visible
- risks are explicit, not buried
- recommendation follows directly from the analysis

## Common failure modes
- too much market data, no investment view
- target economics not tested
- upside asserted, not supported
- risks generic or incomplete
- recommendation not clearly tied to findings
