# Corporate strategy

## When to use this skill
Use for strategy refresh, long-range plan, enterprise repositioning, CEO / board agenda, or translating ambition into a clear set of priorities and initiatives. Do not use for a narrow functional strategy, a pure operating model exercise, or a one-off market scan.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components that the rest of the deck follows: answer / recommendation, strategic messages, value or positioning implication, and execution direction.

## Typical output
Typical length: 10–16 pages in a standard version. This is the default for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the whole deck  
**What it includes:** answer, 3–5 messages, implication, execution direction  
**Typical formats:** message page; pillar summary; strategy house

### 1) Baseline and context — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Where the company stands and why strategy is being revisited  
**What it includes:** current position, performance, portfolio, key issues, strategic context  
**Typical formats:** baseline snapshot; SWOT; issue tree; performance summary

### 2) Outside-in perspective — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Market, customer, competitor, and ecosystem view that informs the strategy  
**What it includes:** market dynamics, customer shifts, competitor moves, peer perspective, external inflections  
**Typical formats:** benchmark table; peer comparison; market map; trend summary

### 3) Strategic choices — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Core choices the company should make  
**What it includes:** where to play, how to win, what to prioritize, what to deprioritize  
**Typical formats:** 2x2; choice matrix; portfolio view; options comparison

### 4) Strategic pillars — 1–4 pages
**What it is:** Few enterprise priorities that define the strategy  
**What it includes:** 3–5 pillars, rationale, key moves under each  
**Typical formats:** pillar page; strategy house; initiative tree

### 5) Initiative portfolio and value logic — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Translation of strategy into action and impact  
**What it includes:** key initiatives, prioritization, value logic, quick wins vs. longer-term bets  
**Typical formats:** prioritization matrix; value bridge; portfolio heatmap

### 6) Roadmap and execution — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the strategy will be delivered over time  
**What it includes:** sequencing, milestones, owners, major dependencies  
**Typical formats:** roadmap; phased plan; chevron with sub-steps

### 7) KPIs and governance — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How strategy delivery will be tracked and governed  
**What it includes:** KPIs, review cadence, accountabilities, escalation logic  
**Typical formats:** KPI scorecard; governance chart; cadence view

## How to build
- clarify the strategic question and decision to be made
- build the inside-out and outside-in fact base
- synthesize implications from the baseline
- define the core choices
- group them into 3–5 strategic pillars
- translate pillars into initiatives, sequencing, and governance
- write the executive summary last, place it first

## Typical data needed
company performance and financials; portfolio / business mix; market and segment data; competitor / peer information; customer insights; leadership ambition and hypotheses; current initiatives and investment plans; major constraints and risks

## Success criteria
Clear answer early; explicit choices; 3–5 sharp pillars; strategy linked to actions; credible execution logic; whole deck traceable from the one-page executive summary.

## Common failure modes
Too much diagnosis, not enough choice; vague or overlapping pillars; too many priorities; no value logic; no sequencing rationale; KPIs disconnected from the strategy.
