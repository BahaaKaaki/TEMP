# Cost optimization / efficiency

## When to use this skill
Use when the ask is to reduce the cost base, improve productivity, or unlock efficiency without undermining core service or growth ambitions. Typical asks include Opex reduction, productivity improvement, zero-based challenge, SG&A efficiency, and function or service cost reset. Do not use when the scope is purely procurement or purely organization design.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as baseline cost view, main levers, savings ambition, implementation logic, and risk to service.

## Typical output
Typical length: 10–16 pages in a standard version. This is the default structure for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the efficiency deck  
**What it includes:** cost baseline, key levers, savings ambition, risks, roadmap  
**Typical formats:** message page; savings summary; recommendation page

### 1) Cost baseline and ambition — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** What the cost base looks like and what the program must achieve  
**What it includes:** scope, spend baseline, cost drivers, ambition, constraints  
**Typical formats:** cost bridge; spend heatmap; baseline table

### 2) Benchmark and root-cause diagnosis — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Where inefficiencies sit and why  
**What it includes:** benchmarks, process issues, organization issues, demand issues, policy issues  
**Typical formats:** benchmark bars; process heatmap; issue tree

### 3) Efficiency levers — 2–4 pages
**What it is:** The major ways to reduce cost or improve productivity  
**What it includes:** levers by category or function, no-regret moves, structural moves  
**Typical formats:** lever tree; category heatmap; operating model page

### 4) Initiative portfolio — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Specific actions required to capture the value  
**What it includes:** initiative list, owners, dependencies, implementation logic  
**Typical formats:** initiative cards; charter page; portfolio view

### 5) Savings sizing and phasing — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** How much can be captured and when  
**What it includes:** gross vs net savings, one-off costs, run-rate timing, phasing  
**Typical formats:** savings waterfall; phased bridge; wave chart

### 6) Delivery model and governance — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the program will be managed  
**What it includes:** PMO, governance, tracking, approval gates, reporting  
**Typical formats:** governance chart; PMO cadence; KPI scorecard

### 7) Risks, people implications, and roadmap — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What could block delivery and how to land the change  
**What it includes:** service continuity, stakeholder resistance, people implications, roadmap  
**Typical formats:** risk matrix; roadmap; action plan

## How to build
- set the ambition and define what costs are in scope
- build a fact-based cost baseline by category, owner, and driver
- benchmark where useful and identify structural root causes
- define the efficiency levers and initiative set
- size gross and net savings with phasing
- translate levers into an execution program with owners and governance
- protect service levels and manage workforce or stakeholder implications

## Success criteria
The baseline is granular, savings are net and time-phased, levers are credible, owners are clear, and service or execution risks are explicitly managed.

## Common failure modes
Across-the-board cuts; double counting; no owner or phasing; missing baseline; gross savings presented as net; no view on service impact or implementation effort.
