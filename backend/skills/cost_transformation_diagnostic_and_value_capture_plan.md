# Cost transformation diagnostic and value capture plan

## When to use this skill
Use this when the client needs an end-to-end efficiency diagnostic that goes beyond procurement and identifies structural, process, technology, and organization levers. Trigger phrases include “cost optimization,” “opex program,” “efficiency program,” “fit for growth,” and “value capture.” Do **not** use it for a narrow sourcing wave or category strategy only; use this skill when leadership wants a fact-based savings case, a prioritized initiative set, and a governance model to realize benefits.

## Deliverable shape
1. **Executive summary with savings case**  
   Purpose: state the savings ambition, the main sources of value, and the effort required.  
   Typical length: 2–3 slides.  
   Complete when: the deck shows a gross-to-net range, top levers, cost-to-achieve view if material, and immediate leadership asks.  
   Common failure modes: vague “efficiency potential,” no distinction between savings types, no owner logic.

2. **Verified cost baseline and scope boundary**  
   Purpose: define the spend / cost perimeter and normalize the baseline.  
   Typical length: 2–3 slides.  
   Complete when: the baseline is reconciled, adjusted for one-offs, and segmented by cost center, function, category, or activity.  
   Common failure modes: disputed baseline, mixing opex with capex without logic, no inflation/volume normalization.

3. **Cost driver diagnostic**  
   Purpose: show what is actually driving cost and where structural inefficiency sits.  
   Typical length: 3–5 slides.  
   Complete when: key drivers by process, org layer, asset footprint, vendor spend, labor model, or service model are visible.  
   Common failure modes: spend cube without implication, no process or organization lens, no distinction between controllable and fixed cost.

4. **Outside-in benchmarks and best practices**  
   Purpose: calibrate ambition and expose where the client is structurally off-market.  
   Typical length: 2–4 slides.  
   Complete when: benchmarks are relevant and linked to specific levers (not generic “top quartile” claims).  
   Common failure modes: incomparable peers, stale benchmarks, no translation from benchmark to opportunity.

5. **Opportunity longlist and lever library**  
   Purpose: translate the diagnosis into actionable levers.  
   Typical length: 2–4 slides.  
   Complete when: opportunities cover policy/process, workforce, asset footprint, vendors, digital/automation, and operating model where relevant.  
   Common failure modes: all levers are procurement levers, no structural moves, no linkage to cost line.

6. **Initiative prioritization and quantified cases**  
   Purpose: move from longlist to a realistic program.  
   Typical length: 3–5 slides.  
   Complete when: each major initiative has owner, timing, estimated impact, dependencies, and implementation difficulty.  
   Common failure modes: no prioritization, impact double-counting, no cost-to-achieve view.

7. **Operating model, policy, and digital enablers**  
   Purpose: show what must change to sustain savings.  
   Typical length: 1–3 slides.  
   Complete when: policy changes, governance changes, digital use cases, and target operating model implications are stated.  
   Common failure modes: savings divorced from operating changes, digital wish list with no economics.

8. **Benefits governance and tracking model**  
   Purpose: define how savings will be challenged, signed off, and tracked.  
   Typical length: 1–2 slides.  
   Complete when: taxonomy, ownership, sign-off rules, reporting cadence, and issue escalation are explicit.  
   Common failure modes: no clear benefit-owner model, savings tracked only at program level, not by initiative.

9. **Roadmap, waves, and PMO structure**  
   Purpose: turn the diagnostic into an execution program.  
   Typical length: 2–3 slides.  
   Complete when: the roadmap separates quick wins from deeper structural moves and defines cadence, forums, and milestones.  
   Common failure modes: all savings put in year one, no wave logic, no program office.

10. **Risk register and management actions**  
Purpose: highlight execution risk and leadership interventions required.  
Typical length: 1–2 slides.  
Complete when: data-quality, stakeholder resistance, digital dependency, and service-level risks are explicit.  
Common failure modes: generic risk list, no service continuity lens.

## Visual framework library
- **Baseline — cost waterfall**: total cost → excluded items → normalized baseline → addressable cost. Use to settle the debate on scope early.
- **Diagnostic — spend heatmap / cost driver tree**: show cost by function/category/process with red-hot cells for outliers. Use a driver tree when leadership needs to understand causality, not just spend concentration.
- **Workforce — spans & layers charts**: manager-to-staff ratios, layer distribution, and benchmark comparisons. Use when labor cost is material and organization redesign is on the table.
- **Footprint / asset base — utilization vs cost scatter**: sites or assets plotted by cost and utilization to surface rationalization candidates.
- **Opportunity library — lever taxonomy page**: policy/process, labor, vendor, asset, digital, demand management. Use to prove coverage beyond sourcing.
- **Prioritization — impact × ease 2x2 plus wave tags**: use for portfolio communication; maintain a hidden model for precise timing and dependencies.
- **Savings — gross-to-net waterfall**: gross opportunity → leakage / reinvestment / cost to achieve → net impact.
- **Benefits tracker — KPI dashboard**: initiative-level target, committed savings, realized savings, owner, status, and issues. Use in weekly PMO rhythm.
- **Roadmap — wave plan with governance forums**: Wave 1 quick wins, Wave 2 structural moves, Wave 3 digital / policy / operating model changes.

## House conventions
- **Action-title format**: titles should quantify or prioritize, e.g., “Three structural levers account for over half of addressable savings.”
- **Executive summary structure**: savings case, top levers, implementation burden, and the first decisions required.
- **How findings are articulated**: cost fact → inefficiency insight → lever → savings implication.
- **Tone**: hard-edged but practical; cost packs should not read like procurement brochures.
- **Vocabulary**: “addressable baseline,” “net savings,” “cost avoidance,” “cost efficiency,” “cost to achieve,” “owner sign-off,” “quick wins,” “structural levers,” “benefits tracking.”
- **Benefit taxonomy**: explicitly separate net savings, efficiency, and avoidance, and be consistent across the entire deck.

## Inputs the skill needs
- **Cost baseline** (**Required**): spend and/or P&L by function/category/site/process; enough to reconcile the scope.
- **Operational data** (**Required**): volumes, utilization, service levels, headcount, or asset metrics.
- **Current initiatives** (**Required**): ongoing cost, digital, procurement, org, or policy efforts to avoid double counting.
- **Benchmark inputs** (**Optional but high value**): peers, industry norms, spans & layers, asset productivity, service levels.
- **Leadership hypotheses** (**Optional**): known sacred cows or early savings ideas.
- **Constraints** (**Required**): service continuity requirements, union/labor constraints, policy commitments, regulatory boundaries.
- **Execution calendar** (**Optional**): budgeting cycle, board dates, transformation office cadence.

## Methodology anchors
- Normalize the baseline before sizing anything; volume growth, inflation, and one-offs can distort the answer materially.
- Separate structural savings from temporary cuts; leadership must know which levers recur and which do not.
- Attach every major lever to an owner and a cost line; otherwise the program will not survive challenge.
- Digital and automation levers should show prerequisites, rollout logic, and benefit timing, not just high-level upside.
- Use benchmarks to calibrate ambition, not to justify arbitrary top-down targets.
- Benefits governance matters as much as the diagnostic; most savings programs fail in realization, not in ideation.
- Protect service levels explicitly where the client is regulated or public-facing.

## Done-state checklist
- The baseline is reconciled and not still being debated.
- Savings are categorized consistently into net savings, efficiency, and avoidance.
- The top opportunities are tied to owners, timing, and dependencies.
- The portfolio is not just procurement; structural and digital levers are present where relevant.
- Quick wins and structural moves are separated.
- The roadmap does not assume unrealistic year-one capture.
- A PMO / challenge cadence and benefit sign-off model are defined.
- Risks to service continuity or stakeholder acceptance are explicit.
- The pack can answer “How much is real, by when, and with what effort?”
- The appendix contains the calculation logic behind the headline numbers.

## Composition notes
This skill commonly composes with **Weekly SteerCo / PMO status deck** and **Final client readout / board decision deck**. Use **Target operating model – design and activation blueprint** when the cost program requires structural redesign of the organization rather than just initiative implementation.

## Metadata
- **Primary sectors:** cross-sector, especially infrastructure-heavy sectors  
- **Typical engagement duration:** 6–12 weeks for diagnostic; longer for realization support  
- **Typical team size:** 4–8 consultants plus analytics / procurement / digital SMEs  
- **Primary consumer seniority:** manager / partner  
- **Decay risk:** medium  
- **Suggested owner discipline:** operations and cost transformation
