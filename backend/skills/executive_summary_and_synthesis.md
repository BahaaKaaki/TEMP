# Executive summary and synthesis

## When to use this skill
Use when the ask is specifically for the executive summary page, synthesis page, board-style summary, or one-page readout that must stand alone. This skill is for the summary page itself, not the broader storyline across the full deck. Do not use for an agenda page disguised as an executive summary.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 4–6 components such as the answer, the context, the 3–5 key pillars, the quantified proof, and the ask or implication.

## Typical output
Typical length: 1 page in a standard version. This is the default structure; longer decks should still keep the executive summary to one page and put additional detail in the body.

### 0) Headline answer — 1 pages
**What it is:** The opening statement of what the deck is saying  
**What it includes:** answer, quantified implication if available, decision direction  
**Typical formats:** message title; recommendation line

### 1) Context and stakes — 1 pages
**What it is:** The short setup for why the issue matters  
**What it includes:** situation, complication, urgency, decision context  
**Typical formats:** context bridge; short problem statement

### 2) Deck-tracking pillar bullets — 1 pages
**What it is:** The core bullets that mirror the body of the deck  
**What it includes:** 3–5 pillar messages, phrased as findings or recommendations  
**Typical formats:** bullet synthesis; storyboard tracker

### 3) Key proof points — 1 pages
**What it is:** The few numbers or facts that make the summary convincing  
**What it includes:** most important metrics, benchmarks, or quantified impacts  
**Typical formats:** KPI callout; mini-waterfall; number tiles

### 4) Implication and ask — 1 pages
**What it is:** What the reader should conclude or approve  
**What it includes:** decision, next step, implication for the client  
**Typical formats:** decision box; action note

## How to build
- state the answer in the opening line, not halfway down the page
- preview the rest of the deck using 3–5 bullets that mirror the body exactly
- make each bullet a message, not a topic label
- use only the most decision-relevant numbers or facts
- keep the page self-standing so a senior reader can stop there and still understand the conclusion
- ensure the wording matches the body sections so the deck feels tracked
- close with the implication, ask, or decision required

## Success criteria
The page stands alone, the answer is explicit, the bullets mirror the rest of the deck, and the reader can predict the body from the summary.

## Common failure modes
It reads like an agenda; it repeats section labels; there is no clear answer; too many bullets; the body does not match the summary; the page is text-heavy but insight-light.
