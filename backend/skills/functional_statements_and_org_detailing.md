# Functional statements and org detailing

## When to use this skill
Use after high-level structure decisions are made and the organization needs to be detailed for activation, hiring, or transition. Do not use as the first step if the core structure and governance choices are still unresolved.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as design basis, detailed structure, functional mandates, role implications, and activation direction.

## Typical output
Typical length: 6–12 pages in a standard version. Use this as the default for a broad ask; narrower asks may focus on one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the org-detailing deck  
**What it includes:** answer, 3–5 messages, detailing implication, activation direction  
**Typical formats:** message page; pillar summary; structure snapshot

### 1) Design basis and scope — 1 page
**What it is:** What structure is being detailed and to what level  
**What it includes:** approved structure, scope boundaries, principles for detailing  
**Typical formats:** context page; scope map; guardrails

### 2) Structural decomposition — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the structure cascades below the top level  
**What it includes:** L1/L2/L3 or equivalent breakdown, core groupings, major reporting lines  
**Typical formats:** org chart; decomposition tree; hierarchy view

### 3) Functional statements — 2–4 pages
**What it is:** What each function exists to do  
**What it includes:** mandate, scope, accountabilities, interfaces, key outputs by function  
**Typical formats:** function sheet; charter page; role map

### 4) Positional and interface detailing — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How positions and interfaces are clarified  
**What it includes:** leadership roles, critical positions, overlaps, interaction points, decision touchpoints  
**Typical formats:** RACI; interface map; role cards

### 5) Activation pack — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What is needed to operationalize the design  
**What it includes:** role priorities, hiring or transition implications, documentation set, handover needs  
**Typical formats:** checklist page; phased plan; activation tracker

## How to build
- confirm the approved structure, scope, and level of detail needed
- define a clear decomposition from L1 to the required depth
- write functional statements and role boundaries consistently
- clarify interfaces, overlaps, and decision touchpoints
- prepare the detail needed for activation and handover

## Success criteria
Functions are clearly defined; overlaps are reduced; structure is detailed enough to activate; interfaces are explicit; language is consistent across functions.

## Common failure modes
Functional statements are generic; detailing is inconsistent; roles still overlap; too much prose and not enough structure; activation needs are ignored.
