# Governance and decision rights

## When to use this skill
Use when decisions are slow, unclear, duplicated, or overly centralized; for holding–subsidiary models; for board and committee design; or where cross-entity interaction needs to be formalized. Do not use for a pure org-chart exercise unless decision rights are a major issue.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as governance problem, target model, forums and authorities, interaction implications, and implementation direction.

## Typical output
Typical length: 8–12 pages in a standard version. Use this as the default for a broad ask; narrower asks may focus on one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the governance deck  
**What it includes:** answer, 3–5 messages, governance implication, implementation direction  
**Typical formats:** message page; pillar summary; governance snapshot

### 1) Governance problem statement — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What governance issues are slowing or distorting performance  
**What it includes:** decision bottlenecks, overlap, weak escalation, unclear interfaces  
**Typical formats:** issue tree; heatmap; pain-point summary

### 2) Governance principles and scope — 1 page
**What it is:** The rules shaping the target governance model  
**What it includes:** principles, boundaries, entities in scope, decisions in scope  
**Typical formats:** principles page; guardrails; scope map

### 3) Target governance model — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** How governance should work in the future  
**What it includes:** boards, committees, forums, interaction model, internal/external interfaces  
**Typical formats:** governance map; forum map; interaction model

### 4) Decision rights and authorities — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Who decides what and at what level  
**What it includes:** DoA logic, major authorities, approval thresholds, RACI or similar  
**Typical formats:** authority matrix; RACI; decision tree

### 5) Reporting and cadence — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How governance becomes operational  
**What it includes:** review forums, reporting rhythm, escalation paths, packs and triggers  
**Typical formats:** cadence view; reporting pyramid; governance calendar

### 6) Implementation and change implications — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What it takes to activate the new model  
**What it includes:** policy updates, charter updates, socialization, training, handover  
**Typical formats:** roadmap; phased plan; checklist page

## How to build
- define the decisions and interfaces that matter most
- diagnose governance pain points and unclear authorities
- set governance principles and role boundaries
- design the forum structure, charters, and decision-rights logic
- translate the model into practical matrices and cadence
- sequence implementation and handover

## Success criteria
Decision ownership is explicit; committees have a real purpose; escalation paths are clear; internal and external interfaces are practical; governance is light enough to work.

## Common failure modes
Too many committees; decision rights stay vague; governance separated from structure and process; no escalation logic; model is too theoretical.
