# Governance cadence / decision forums

## When to use this skill
Use when the client needs clear SteerCo, ExCom, board, or working-forum design to govern a program or portfolio. Typical asks include governance models, decision rights, committee architecture, cadence calendars, and escalation design. Do not use for the broader operating model or for a generic PMO deck with no forum-design question at its core.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as governance layers, forum mandates, decision rights, cadence, and supporting artifacts.

## Typical output
Typical length: 8–12 pages in a standard version. This is the default structure for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the governance design  
**What it includes:** layers, forums, rights, cadence, closure model  
**Typical formats:** message page; governance summary; decision page

### 1) Governance principles and layers — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the governance model is organized from working level to leadership  
**What it includes:** design principles, layers, relationship between strategic and working forums  
**Typical formats:** governance pyramid; layered map; principles page

### 2) Forum map and mandates — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What forums exist and what each is for  
**What it includes:** forum list, mandate, scope, participants, decisions owned  
**Typical formats:** committee map; mandate table; governance map

### 3) Decision rights and escalation — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Who decides what and what happens when issues cannot be resolved  
**What it includes:** decision rights, approvals, thresholds, escalation path, stage gates  
**Typical formats:** decision-rights matrix; stage gate; escalation flow

### 4) Cadence calendar — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How often each forum meets and how the cycle hangs together  
**What it includes:** calendar, timing, sequencing of working-level and leadership-level forums  
**Typical formats:** calendar view; cadence map; meeting ladder

### 5) Pre-read, meeting, and output standards — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What good inputs and outputs look like  
**What it includes:** pre-read expectations, pack structure, agenda logic, resolution capture, follow-up  
**Typical formats:** meeting template; agenda page; output checklist

### 6) Decision closure and follow-up — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How decisions translate into action and accountability  
**What it includes:** decision log, action tracking, owner follow-up, unresolved-item handling  
**Typical formats:** decision log; action tracker; closure workflow

## How to build
- identify the key decisions the program needs to make
- define the governance layers and forum logic around those decisions
- assign decision rights, escalation paths, and ownership
- set a workable cadence with inputs and outputs for each forum
- standardize pre-read, meeting, and follow-up artifacts
- close the loop with decision logging and action closure rules

## Success criteria
Forum design is simple, mandates are clear, escalation is fast, and every meeting has a defined purpose, input, output, and owner.

## Common failure modes
Too many forums; duplicate mandates; vague approval rights; calendar not linked to decision needs; no closure mechanism after meetings.
