# Growth strategy

## When to use this skill
Use for sustained growth agenda, beyond-the-core growth, revenue acceleration, new business lines, adjacency moves, or integrated growth plans. Do not use for a full corporate strategy refresh unless growth is only one pillar of a broader strategy.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as growth diagnosis, priority growth vectors, core growth plays, value impact, and execution direction.

## Typical output
Typical length: 10–16 pages in a standard version. This is the default for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the growth deck  
**What it includes:** growth answer, 3–5 messages, value implication, execution direction  
**Typical formats:** message page; pillar summary; growth thesis page

### 1) Current growth baseline — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Where growth is coming from today and where the gap sits  
**What it includes:** historical growth, mix, growth drivers, pressure points  
**Typical formats:** performance summary; growth bridge; mix view

### 2) Growth pools and opportunity spaces — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Where the business can grow faster  
**What it includes:** customer, segment, geography, product, channel, adjacency, or inorganic pools  
**Typical formats:** market map; segment view; whitespace map

### 3) Strategic growth choices — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Which growth vectors to back  
**What it includes:** organic vs inorganic, core vs beyond-core, priority bets, trade-offs  
**Typical formats:** 2x2; option screen; portfolio view

### 4) Growth plays — 1–4 pages
**What it is:** How the chosen growth vectors will be won  
**What it includes:** target plays, proposition shifts, channels, pricing, partnerships, acquisitions, capability needs  
**Typical formats:** play cards; initiative tree; value proposition matrix

### 5) Value logic and prioritization — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Impact and relative attractiveness of the plays  
**What it includes:** value pools, investment needs, prioritization, quick wins vs longer bets  
**Typical formats:** prioritization matrix; value bridge; bubble chart

### 6) Roadmap and enablers — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How growth will be delivered  
**What it includes:** sequencing, ownership, critical enablers, governance  
**Typical formats:** roadmap; phased plan; chevron with sub-steps

### 7) KPIs and governance — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How growth performance will be tracked  
**What it includes:** growth KPIs, review cadence, accountability  
**Typical formats:** KPI scorecard; governance chart; cadence view

## How to build
- quantify current growth profile and growth gap
- size growth pools by segment, product, channel, geography, or adjacency
- screen growth options and choose the priority vectors
- define the growth plays and required enablers
- translate plays into initiative portfolio, value logic, and roadmap
- close with governance and KPI logic

## Typical data needed
historical revenue and margin trends; customer and segment mix; market growth and share data; competitor moves; channel and pricing data; pipeline or opportunity data; M&A / partnership hypotheses; constraints and investment appetite

## Success criteria
Growth gap is explicit; growth vectors are prioritized; plays are concrete; value logic is visible; execution path is credible.

## Common failure modes
List of ideas instead of choices; no quantified growth pools; no distinction between core and beyond-core; no enablers; no value bridge or sequencing.
