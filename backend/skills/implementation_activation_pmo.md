# Implementation / activation / PMO

## When to use this skill
Use when a strategy or transformation is moving into execution and the client needs PMO, TMO, SMO, or implementation-office setup and activation. Typical asks include portfolio oversight, initiative monitoring, execution governance, toolkits, and first-wave activation. Do not use for the strategy design itself or for a pure board-reporting pack.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as PMO mandate, portfolio view, governance and reporting, risks and dependencies, and the first activation wave.

## Typical output
Typical length: 10–16 pages in a standard version. This is the default structure for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the implementation office  
**What it includes:** mandate, portfolio status, key risks, cadence, activation priorities  
**Typical formats:** message page; PMO summary; leadership dashboard

### 1) Mandate and success model — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What the PMO or TMO is there to do and what good looks like  
**What it includes:** mandate, scope, exclusions, principles, success criteria  
**Typical formats:** mandate page; scope box; success-model page

### 2) Office model and roles — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the office is structured and staffed  
**What it includes:** PMO model, roles, interfaces with sponsors and workstreams, staffing logic  
**Typical formats:** org chart; role map; coverage model

### 3) Portfolio and workstream architecture — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How delivery is organized and monitored  
**What it includes:** portfolio structure, workstreams, initiative groupings, interdependencies  
**Typical formats:** portfolio map; workstream map; dependency view

### 4) Governance, reporting, and escalation — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How decisions, reporting, and issue escalation work  
**What it includes:** forums, reporting layers, escalation paths, stage gates, approvals  
**Typical formats:** governance pyramid; reporting ladder; escalation flow

### 5) Risk, issue, dependency, and action management — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the office keeps control of delivery friction  
**What it includes:** RAID approach, thresholds, mitigation process, action follow-up  
**Typical formats:** RAID dashboard; issue heatmap; action tracker

### 6) Tools, templates, and dashboards — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What standard artifacts support execution  
**What it includes:** templates, dashboards, registers, knowledge repository, workflow logic  
**Typical formats:** dashboard; toolkit page; workflow map

### 7) Activation plan and first 90 days — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the office is launched and stabilized  
**What it includes:** mobilization steps, early milestones, first reviews, handover logic if required  
**Typical formats:** 90-day plan; milestone calendar; activation roadmap

## How to build
- define the office mandate, scope, and success criteria
- choose the right PMO or TMO model and resourcing shape
- translate the strategy into portfolio and workstream controls
- set governance, reporting, RAID, and escalation routines
- deploy tools, templates, and dashboards
- launch the cadence and support the first execution wave
- prepare handover or capability transfer logic where needed

## Success criteria
The office has a clear mandate, reporting is decision-useful, risks and dependencies are surfaced early, and the first delivery wave is operational rather than theoretical.

## Common failure modes
PMO becomes an admin layer; no clarity on role vs workstreams; reporting is activity-heavy and decision-light; tools are generic; activation has no first-wave plan.
