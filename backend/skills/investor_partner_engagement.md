# Investor / partner engagement

## When to use this skill
Use when a strategy or opportunity must be supported by investor attraction, partner outreach, alliance-building, or a structured engagement funnel. Do not use for a pure internal stakeholder plan.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as target counterparties, engagement thesis, outreach model, pipeline governance, and implementation direction.

## Typical output
Typical length: 8–14 pages in a standard version. This is the default for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the investor / partner deck  
**What it includes:** engagement answer, 3–5 messages, opportunity implication, execution direction  
**Typical formats:** message page; engagement framework; partner summary

### 1) Objective and target counterparty logic — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Why engagement is happening and with whom  
**What it includes:** engagement objective, target investor or partner archetypes, fit logic  
**Typical formats:** objective page; archetype map; segmentation table

### 2) Opportunity and engagement thesis — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Why a counterparty should engage  
**What it includes:** value proposition, strategic rationale, partnership logic, opportunity framing  
**Typical formats:** thesis page; value proposition canvas; opportunity summary

### 3) Targeting and prioritization — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Which targets matter most  
**What it includes:** priority list, screening logic, outreach tiers, relationship logic  
**Typical formats:** targeting matrix; heatmap; 2x2

### 4) Outreach model and funnel — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** How engagement will progress over time  
**What it includes:** contact strategy, campaign logic, funnel stages, milestones, conversion points  
**Typical formats:** funnel; journey map; outreach model

### 5) Collateral, meetings, and roadshow logic — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What engagement assets and moments are needed  
**What it includes:** pitch materials, meetings, roadshows, workshops, partner sessions  
**Typical formats:** campaign map; event plan; collateral stack

### 6) Pipeline governance and tracking — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How progress will be managed  
**What it includes:** owners, trackers, cadence, escalation, decision points  
**Typical formats:** pipeline tracker; governance chart; cadence view

### 7) Roadmap and next steps — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the engagement effort will be sequenced  
**What it includes:** phases, quick wins, milestones, dependencies  
**Typical formats:** roadmap; phased plan; action tracker

## How to build
- clarify the objective of engagement and the type of counterparty needed
- segment and prioritize investor or partner targets
- build the engagement proposition and outreach model
- define the funnel from awareness to active engagement
- set pipeline governance, tracking, and next-step logic

## Success criteria
The target audience is prioritized, the engagement proposition is credible, outreach is structured, and the engagement funnel is governed like a pipeline rather than ad hoc networking.

## Common failure modes
Long target list with no prioritization; generic pitch; no funnel stages; no tracking; no governance or ownership of next steps.
