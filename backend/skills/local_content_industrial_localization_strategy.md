# Local content / industrial localization strategy

## When to use this skill
Use this when a government, sector custodian, sovereign-backed platform, or industrial champion needs a programmatic answer on how to localize products, services, suppliers, workforce, and technology. Typical trigger phrases include “local content,” “localization,” “in-country value,” “supplier development,” “technology transfer,” and “industrialization strategy.” Do **not** use it for a product-level local-content calculation methodology or a one-off supplier audit; this skill is for strategy and program design.

## Deliverable shape
1. **Executive summary and localization thesis**  
   Purpose: state what should be localized first, why, and what the program is trying to achieve.  
   Typical length: 2–3 slides.  
   Complete when: priority categories, value logic, and the recommended program architecture are explicit.  
   Common failure modes: policy aspirations without category focus, no prioritization, no implementation stance.

2. **Baseline of current local content and value chain**  
   Purpose: establish where spend/value currently leaks and how the value chain works.  
   Typical length: 2–4 slides.  
   Complete when: categories, value-chain tiers, import dependency, supplier base, workforce, and technology baseline are visible.  
   Common failure modes: spend-only baseline, no tier view, no distinction between goods, services, and technology.

3. **Opportunity identification and category prioritization**  
   Purpose: decide what should be localized first.  
   Typical length: 3–5 slides.  
   Complete when: categories are screened on competitiveness, localization ease, strategic importance, and socio-economic value.  
   Common failure modes: trying to localize everything, prioritizing politically visible categories with weak economics, no screening framework.

4. **Target plays by category / cluster / sub-sector**  
   Purpose: translate priority categories into specific local plays.  
   Typical length: 2–4 slides.  
   Complete when: category-specific opportunities, required capabilities, target suppliers/OEMs, and route-to-localization are defined.  
   Common failure modes: broad sector statements with no product/service specificity.

5. **Enablers and incentives design**  
   Purpose: define what the ecosystem must provide to make localization stick.  
   Typical length: 2–4 slides.  
   Complete when: land, finance, duty/tax treatment, energy, grants, procurement preferences, and regulatory enablers are specified.  
   Common failure modes: incentive wishlist, no gap-to-close logic, no benchmark support.

6. **Supplier development, OEM / JV, and investment model**  
   Purpose: show how local capacity is actually built.  
   Typical length: 2–4 slides.  
   Complete when: supplier pipeline, OEM engagement, co-investment logic, JV models, and platform/investment-vehicle options are explicit.  
   Common failure modes: assuming suppliers emerge without commercial anchors, no route to market, no OEM logic.

7. **Workforce localization and technology transfer agenda**  
   Purpose: define how capability moves onshore.  
   Typical length: 2–3 slides.  
   Complete when: workforce, training, commercialization, R&D, and technology-transfer mechanisms are addressed.  
   Common failure modes: workforce and technology transfer collapsed into one vague page.

8. **Program governance and operating model**  
   Purpose: specify how the localization program will be run and measured.  
   Typical length: 1–3 slides.  
   Complete when: sponsor role, delivery office, certification / analytics / monitoring model, and stakeholder interfaces are clear.  
   Common failure modes: program run purely as policy, no data platform, no accountability.

9. **Socio-economic impact and KPI framework**  
   Purpose: quantify why the program matters and how progress is tracked.  
   Typical length: 1–3 slides.  
   Complete when: jobs, GDP, export potential, import substitution, and local-content progression metrics are linked to categories and initiatives.  
   Common failure modes: headline job claims with no logic, no category-level KPIs.

10. **Roadmap, pilots, and scale-up**  
Purpose: phase the localization journey into something executable.  
Typical length: 2–3 slides.  
Complete when: quick wins, pilot categories, enabling reforms, and scale-up waves are sequenced.  
Common failure modes: no pilots, no sequencing of enablers, no sponsor actions in year one.

## Visual framework library
- **Baseline — value-chain pyramid**: end markets at top, intermediate products in middle, raw materials/components at bottom. Use when the strategy must show where import leakage occurs.
- **Prioritization — competitiveness × ease-of-localization matrix**: use to rank categories; add bubble size for socio-economic impact if the audience wants a one-page synthesis.
- **Opportunity mapping — tiered supplier ecosystem chart**: OEMs, Tier 1/2/3 suppliers, service providers. Use when supplier-development design matters.
- **Incentive design — gap-closing table**: current economics, localization gap, proposed enablers, responsible entity. Use instead of a generic incentive list.
- **Supplier development — pipeline funnel**: target opportunities → qualified suppliers → pilot partners → scaled suppliers.
- **Technology transfer — commercialization pathway**: research / transfer / pilot / manufacturing / scale. Useful when the ask covers more than goods localization.
- **Impact — socio-economic waterfall**: import substitution, local spend, jobs, GDP, export upside.
- **Program model — localization office / platform operating model**: strategy, analytics, supplier development, incentives, certification, reporting.
- **Roadmap — category waves**: Wave 1 pilot categories, Wave 2 scale-up, Wave 3 deeper technology transfer.

## House conventions
- **Action-title format**: titles should state a localization choice, e.g., “The first wave should focus on categories with high demand certainty and manageable localization complexity.”
- **Executive summary structure**: what to localize first, what enablers matter, what delivery model to use, and what impact to expect.
- **How findings are articulated**: value-chain fact → localization opportunity → enabling requirement → program implication.
- **Tone**: commercially grounded and implementation-oriented; avoid purely nationalist or policy rhetoric.
- **Vocabulary**: “category localization roadmap,” “supplier development,” “co-investment,” “technology transfer,” “local content certification,” “import substitution,” “OEM engagement,” “incentive package.”
- **Proof discipline**: link each priority category to real demand, not just conceptual appeal.

## Inputs the skill needs
- **Sector / category scope** (**Required**): sector, spend base, value-chain scope, and whether the focus is goods, services, workforce, technology, or all four.
- **Baseline spend / sourcing view** (**Required**): category spend, import dependency, supplier base, current local-content performance.
- **Demand outlook** (**Required**): procurement pipeline, national projects, OEM demand, or other anchor demand.
- **Capability baseline** (**Optional but high value**): local industrial capability, workforce base, R&D assets.
- **Incentive and policy context** (**Required**): what support already exists and where gaps remain.
- **Sponsor role and authority** (**Required**): policy setter, procurer, funder, investor, or regulator.
- **Partner hypotheses** (**Optional**): target OEMs, JV ideas, supplier clusters, or platform concepts.

## Methodology anchors
- Prioritize categories with both demand certainty and feasible localization pathways; symbolic categories with no scale waste program credibility.
- Separate goods/services, workforce, and technology transfer; they move on different timelines and need different instruments.
- Use incentives to close real economic gaps, not as a standard package.
- Supplier development needs route-to-market logic; certification and training alone are not enough.
- Keep the sponsor’s role explicit: regulator, buyer, investor, and delivery office each imply different tools.
- Quantify impact at the category level where possible; aggregate impact without category logic is weak.

## Done-state checklist
- The program names specific categories and value-chain nodes, not just sectors.
- Category prioritization is transparent and evidence-based.
- Enablers are linked to specific economic gaps.
- Supplier/OEM/JV logic is explicit.
- Workforce and technology-transfer agendas are separated and practical.
- The governance model includes data, monitoring, and accountability.
- Impact metrics are credible and not just headline job numbers.
- The roadmap starts with pilot categories and near-term sponsor actions.
- The strategy can answer “Why these categories first?” convincingly.
- The pack is implementable by both policymakers and delivery teams.

## Composition notes
This skill often composes with **Sector development strategy and implementation playbook** and **Strategic business case and feasibility study**. Use the business-case skill for a single platform, plant, or investment vehicle that emerges from the localization strategy; use this skill for the broader localization program itself.

- **Typical engagement duration:** 8–16 weeks  
- **Typical team size:** 4–8 consultants plus industrial / economics SMEs  
- **Primary consumer seniority:** manager / partner  
- **Decay risk:** medium  
- **Suggested owner discipline:** industrial strategy and localization
