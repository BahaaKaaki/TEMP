# Localization / local content strategy

## When to use this skill
Use for local content, localization, national value creation, supply-chain localization, import substitution, or industrial participation agendas. Do not use for a narrow procurement-savings exercise or a generic industrial strategy with no localization focus.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as localization baseline, target opportunities, priority levers, enabling programs, and implementation direction.

## Typical output
Typical length: 12–18 pages in a standard version. This is the default for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the localization deck  
**What it includes:** localization answer, 3–5 messages, impact implication, execution direction  
**Typical formats:** message page; pillar summary; localization framework page

### 1) Localization mandate and baseline — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Current state of local value capture  
**What it includes:** mandate, current spend / value chain, import leakage, pain points  
**Typical formats:** baseline snapshot; spend waterfall; value-chain map

### 2) Benchmark and archetypes — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** How other programs or markets have approached localization  
**What it includes:** benchmark models, policy tools, program archetypes, lessons  
**Typical formats:** benchmark table; peer comparison; archetype view

### 3) Priority opportunities — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Where localization should focus first  
**What it includes:** target segments, products, categories, supplier opportunities, trade-offs  
**Typical formats:** 2x2; opportunity matrix; heatmap

### 4) Localization levers and programs — 2–4 pages
**What it is:** How localization will be unlocked  
**What it includes:** supplier development, investment attraction, incentives, funds, capability build, standards, policy interventions  
**Typical formats:** initiative tree; program portfolio; operating model page

### 5) Delivery model and enablers — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Who will do what and with what support  
**What it includes:** institutional roles, governance, funding logic, partner roles, enabling capabilities  
**Typical formats:** governance chart; operating model page; capability heatmap

### 6) Roadmap and activation — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the strategy will be phased  
**What it includes:** milestones, workstreams, quick wins, major dependencies  
**Typical formats:** roadmap; phased plan; chevron with sub-steps

### 7) KPIs and governance — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How localization progress will be tracked  
**What it includes:** KPI set, governance, review cadence, escalation  
**Typical formats:** KPI scorecard; governance chart; cadence view

## How to build
- define localization objective and scope
- baseline the current spend or value chain and identify leakage
- benchmark relevant localization models and policy tools
- prioritize target segments, products, or opportunities
- define the levers: supplier development, investment attraction, funds, incentives, capability build, policy changes
- translate into roadmap, governance, and KPI logic

## Typical data needed
spend or procurement baseline; import / local split; value-chain map; supplier landscape; benchmark programs; target products or categories; job and value-add logic; funding and policy constraints

## Success criteria
Baseline is quantified; priorities are focused; levers are practical; supplier and investor logic is visible; roadmap and KPIs are implementable.

## Common failure modes
Localization aspiration without quantified baseline; no prioritization; too much policy with no delivery logic; no supplier development path; weak KPI framework.
