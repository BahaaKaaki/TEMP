# Market assessment

## When to use this skill
Use for market scans, entry questions, opportunity identification, and market-based strategy work. Do not use when the ask is target-specific commercial due diligence or a full enterprise strategy.

## Executive summary
One page only. It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 messages around market size and growth, segment attractiveness, competitive structure, whitespace, and implication.

## Typical output
Typical length: 8–12 pages in a standard version. This is the default structure for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page market storyline tracker  
**What it includes:** answer, 3–5 messages, implication for the client  
**Typical formats:** message page, summary strip, market snapshot

### 1) Market definition and scope — 1 page
**What it is:** What market is being assessed  
**What it includes:** scope, boundaries, products / services, geographies, segments  
**Typical formats:** scope page, market tree, definition page

### 2) Market size and growth — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** How big the market is and where it is heading  
**What it includes:** market size, historic growth, forecast, key drivers  
**Typical formats:** market bridge, growth chart, demand curve

### 3) Segmentation and demand drivers — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Where the attractive pockets are  
**What it includes:** customer / segment split, use cases, needs, growth pools  
**Typical formats:** segment tree, heatmap, demand driver page

### 4) Competition and value chain — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the market is structured and who captures value  
**What it includes:** competitors, shares, value chain, routes to market  
**Typical formats:** competitor map, value chain, benchmark table

### 5) Gaps and whitespace — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Where opportunity exists  
**What it includes:** underserved needs, unmet demand, structural gaps, whitespace  
**Typical formats:** gap analysis, whitespace map, priority matrix

### 6) Implications and priorities — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What the client should do with the market view  
**What it includes:** priority segments, focus areas, implications for entry or growth  
**Typical formats:** recommendation page, priority ladder

## How to build
- define the market clearly
- size the market and growth pools
- segment demand and identify key drivers
- map competitors and value chain economics
- identify gaps and attractive pockets
- turn findings into strategic implications

## Success criteria
- clear market definition
- robust sizing and segmentation
- outside-in view is decision-useful
- whitespace is explicit
- implications are sharper than the facts

## Common failure modes
- ambiguous market boundary
- size with no segmentation
- competitor list without structure
- no whitespace conclusion
- implications too generic
