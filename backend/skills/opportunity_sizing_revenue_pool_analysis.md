# Opportunity sizing / revenue pool analysis

## When to use this skill
Use for early-stage opportunity framing, prioritization, revenue-pool mapping, market entry screening, or growth theses. Do not use for a full strategy deck unless the core ask is the size of the opportunity.

## Executive summary
One page only. It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 messages around total pool, segmented pools, addressable share, priority whitespaces, and implication.

## Typical output
Typical length: 6–10 pages in a standard version. This is the default structure for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page sizing storyline tracker  
**What it includes:** answer, 3–5 messages, priority implication  
**Typical formats:** message page, sizing summary, recommendation page

### 1) Scope and sizing logic — 1 page
**What it is:** What is being sized and how  
**What it includes:** definitions, assumptions, logic, scope boundaries  
**Typical formats:** scope page, sizing bridge, issue tree

### 2) Total opportunity pool — 1 page
**What it is:** How large the total market or pool is  
**What it includes:** top-down pool, historic / forecast logic, total value  
**Typical formats:** market bridge, pool chart, summary table

### 3) Segmented pools — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Where value sits within the pool  
**What it includes:** segment, product, channel, geography, customer splits  
**Typical formats:** heatmap, segment tree, waterfall

### 4) Addressable and prioritized pool — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What is realistically addressable and worth pursuing  
**What it includes:** addressable share, priority pockets, whitespaces, trade-offs  
**Typical formats:** priority matrix, whitespace map, addressable bridge

### 5) Implications and recommendation — 1 page
**What it is:** What the sizing means for action  
**What it includes:** focus areas, implications for strategy, next step  
**Typical formats:** recommendation page, priority ladder

## How to build
- define scope and sizing logic
- estimate the total pool
- split the pool into meaningful segments
- determine what is realistically addressable
- prioritize the most attractive pockets
- conclude on implications and next steps

## Success criteria
- sizing logic is clear
- segments are decision-useful
- addressable pool is explicit
- priorities are visible
- output can support action, not just estimation

## Common failure modes
- opaque sizing logic
- too much total market, not enough segmentation
- no addressable view
- no prioritization
- implication does not follow from the sizing
