# Options evaluation and recommendation

## When to use this skill
Use when leadership must choose among strategic, operating, governance, delivery, partnership, or investment options and needs a structured comparison. Typical asks include where-to-play choices, ownership model choices, centralization choices, sourcing choices, and pathway selection. Do not use for a full business case where one option must be fully built out end-to-end.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as the decision frame, evaluation criteria, key trade-offs, recommended option, and implication.

## Typical output
Typical length: 8–14 pages in a standard version. This is the default structure for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the options deck  
**What it includes:** decision, criteria, trade-offs, recommendation, implication  
**Typical formats:** message page; recommendation page; summary scorecard

### 1) Decision frame and criteria — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What choice is being made and how it will be judged  
**What it includes:** decision scope, design principles, criteria, weighting logic if used  
**Typical formats:** decision frame; criteria matrix; guiding-principles page

### 2) Options definition — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Clear definition of the viable choices  
**What it includes:** option descriptions, boundaries, what each option includes and excludes  
**Typical formats:** option cards; archetype page; model comparison

### 3) Comparative assessment — 2–4 pages
**What it is:** How the options perform against the criteria  
**What it includes:** pros and cons, evidence, assessment by criterion, comparative view  
**Typical formats:** heatmap; scorecard; comparison table

### 4) Financial and commercial implications — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** The economic and practical implications of each path  
**What it includes:** cost, value, complexity, timing, scalability, commercial implications  
**Typical formats:** trade-off table; bubble chart; impact vs ease

### 5) Risks and trade-offs — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What could go wrong and what must be accepted  
**What it includes:** key risks, constraints, dependencies, downside considerations  
**Typical formats:** risk matrix; trade-off page; scenario notes

### 6) Recommendation and rationale — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** The answer and why it is preferred  
**What it includes:** recommended option, rationale, why other options are not chosen  
**Typical formats:** recommendation bridge; decision tree; message page

### 7) Implementation implications — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What the recommendation means in practice  
**What it includes:** required actions, enablers, governance, transition implications  
**Typical formats:** roadmap; action plan; chevron with sub-steps

## How to build
- define the decision and what a good answer must optimize for
- set explicit criteria and trade-off logic
- define mutually distinct options
- assess each option against evidence and implications
- make trade-offs visible rather than buried
- recommend one path and state why the others are not preferred
- spell out what the recommendation requires to succeed

## Success criteria
The criteria are explicit, the options are genuinely distinct, the trade-offs are visible, and the recommendation is supported by logic rather than preference.

## Common failure modes
Options overlap; criteria are hidden; scoring looks false-precision; recommendation is timid; negatives of the preferred option are not addressed; next steps are missing.
