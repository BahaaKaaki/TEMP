# Organization design

## When to use this skill
Use for new entities, restructuring, post-merger redesign, growth-stage redesign, centralization or decentralization, or when roles and spans are unclear. Do not use for a pure governance or process exercise unless structural design is central.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as design case for change, organization choices, recommended structure, people implications, and implementation direction.

## Typical output
Typical length: 8–14 pages in a standard version. Use this as the default for a broad ask; narrower asks may focus on one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the organization design deck  
**What it includes:** answer, 3–5 messages, structure implication, implementation direction  
**Typical formats:** message page; pillar summary; target-state snapshot

### 1) Design case for change — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Why the current structure no longer fits the mandate  
**What it includes:** strategic context, pain points, growth/complexity triggers, design objectives  
**Typical formats:** context page; issue tree; pain-point summary

### 2) Design principles and criteria — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What good looks like for the future structure  
**What it includes:** design principles, criteria, constraints, sizing assumptions  
**Typical formats:** principles page; criteria table; design guardrails

### 3) Structural options — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Alternative organization models considered  
**What it includes:** functional vs product vs geographic vs hybrid options, pros/cons, trade-offs  
**Typical formats:** option cards; 2x2; comparison matrix

### 4) Recommended structure — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Preferred organization model and why it wins  
**What it includes:** L1/L2 logic, major groupings, reporting lines, central vs distributed roles  
**Typical formats:** org chart; structure logic page; role grouping view

### 5) Detailing and role implications — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** How the structure translates into management reality  
**What it includes:** layering, spans, sub-functions, accountability boundaries, key interfaces  
**Typical formats:** span-and-layer table; RACI; function map

### 6) Workforce and activation implications — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What changes in staffing and activation  
**What it includes:** key leadership roles, migration implications, key no-regret moves  
**Typical formats:** migration view; phased plan; heatmap

### 7) Governance and implementation — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the structure will be landed and governed  
**What it includes:** decision forums, approvals, sequencing, implementation milestones  
**Typical formats:** roadmap; governance chart; cadence view

## How to build
- clarify mandate, scope, and structural design objectives
- identify the main design imperatives, constraints, and role groupings
- define organization design principles and evaluation criteria
- develop and compare structural options
- select and detail the preferred structure to the needed level
- translate structure into migration and activation steps

## Success criteria
Structure is aligned to priorities; reporting lines and spans are sensible; accountability is clearer; design choices are explicit and defendable; implementation is feasible.

## Common failure modes
Org chart first, logic later; too many layers; overlaps remain unresolved; structure ignores scale and capabilities; no migration path.
