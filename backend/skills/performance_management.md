# Performance management

## When to use this skill
Use for KPI redesign, scorecard design, performance reporting reform, strategy cascading, incentive linkage, or when management lacks a practical rhythm for tracking delivery. Do not use for a broad strategy refresh unless performance management is the specific ask.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as performance question, KPI architecture, target-setting logic, governance and cadence, and implementation direction.

## Typical output
Typical length: 8–14 pages in a standard version. Use this as the default for a broad ask; narrower asks may focus on one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the performance management deck  
**What it includes:** answer, 3–5 messages, performance implication, implementation direction  
**Typical formats:** message page; pillar summary; KPI snapshot

### 1) Performance question and principles — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What performance system is needed and what it should achieve  
**What it includes:** strategic context, pain points, design principles, management intent  
**Typical formats:** context page; issue tree; principles page

### 2) KPI architecture — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** How measures link to strategy and operations  
**What it includes:** strategy map, KPI tree, leading vs lagging indicators, corporate vs functional scorecards  
**Typical formats:** strategy map; KPI tree; scorecard view

### 3) Scorecards and target setting — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** What gets measured and how targets are set  
**What it includes:** scorecard dimensions, metric definitions, target-setting approach, baselines, ownership  
**Typical formats:** Balanced Scorecard; KPI table; target-setting view

### 4) Governance and review cadence — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How performance will be managed in practice  
**What it includes:** review forums, reporting cycle, escalation, corrective-action process  
**Typical formats:** cadence view; governance chart; review calendar

### 5) Incentives, consequences, and follow-through — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the system drives behavior  
**What it includes:** reward linkage where relevant, accountability, action plans, corrective actions  
**Typical formats:** decision tree; action tracker; governance chart

### 6) Dashboards and implementation — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the system will be operationalized  
**What it includes:** dashboard logic, data ownership, rollout steps, training, governance handover  
**Typical formats:** dashboard mock-up; roadmap; phased plan

## How to build
- clarify what performance needs to be managed and why
- link strategic objectives to measurable outcomes and drivers
- define scorecards, KPIs, and ownership
- set target-setting and review logic
- design reporting, escalation, and corrective-action mechanisms
- translate into rollout and governance

## Success criteria
KPIs are tied to objectives; scorecards are manageable; ownership is clear; cadence drives action, not just reporting; incentives and consequences are coherent where relevant.

## Common failure modes
Too many KPIs; measures without decisions; no target-setting logic; dashboards without governance; no clear owner or corrective-action process.
