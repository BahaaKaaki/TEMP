# Policy and regulatory strategy

## When to use this skill
Use for policy reform, regulatory framework design, legislative / regulatory strategy, market-shaping interventions, or institutional policy agendas. Do not use for a narrow legal drafting task or a pure sector strategy unless policy is the main lever.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as policy problem, baseline and gaps, intervention logic, recommended framework, and implementation direction.

## Typical output
Typical length: 12–18 pages in a standard version. This is the default for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the policy deck  
**What it includes:** policy answer, 3–5 messages, impact implication, implementation direction  
**Typical formats:** message page; pillar summary; policy framework page

### 1) Policy mandate and problem statement — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What issue the policy is addressing and why now  
**What it includes:** mandate, problem, outcomes sought, strategic context  
**Typical formats:** context page; issue statement; strategy house

### 2) Baseline and diagnostic — 2–4 pages
**What it is:** Current state of the market, system, or regulatory environment  
**What it includes:** current instruments, structural gaps, bottlenecks, stakeholder pain points  
**Typical formats:** baseline snapshot; issue tree; heatmap

### 3) Benchmark and archetypes — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** How peer jurisdictions address similar issues  
**What it includes:** benchmark models, intervention types, comparative lessons  
**Typical formats:** benchmark table; archetype view; peer comparison

### 4) Policy options and intervention logic — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Possible interventions and trade-offs  
**What it includes:** option set, decision criteria, expected effects, trade-offs  
**Typical formats:** options comparison; 2x2; decision matrix

### 5) Recommended framework — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Preferred policy or regulatory design  
**What it includes:** core instruments, guiding principles, target model, role of institutions  
**Typical formats:** policy framework page; operating model page; strategy house

### 6) Implementation and consultation — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the policy will be socialized and delivered  
**What it includes:** roadmap, consultation, decision gates, ownership  
**Typical formats:** roadmap; consultation map; chevron with sub-steps

### 7) KPIs and governance — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the policy will be monitored  
**What it includes:** KPIs, governance, review cadence  
**Typical formats:** KPI scorecard; governance chart; cadence view

## How to build
- define the policy objective and problem statement
- assess the baseline, current instruments, and institutional context
- benchmark peer regimes and policy archetypes
- develop and screen intervention options
- recommend the preferred framework and implementation path
- close with governance, consultation, and KPI logic

## Typical data needed
policy mandate; current regulations and instruments; baseline market or sector facts; stakeholder views; benchmark jurisdictions; implementation constraints; institutional roles; economic / social impact logic

## Success criteria
Problem is sharply framed; intervention logic is clear; policy choices are explicit; implementation is feasible; roles and KPIs are defined.

## Common failure modes
Policy catalogue instead of strategy; no baseline evidence; vague intervention logic; no institutional ownership; no consultation or implementation path.
