# Policy / public consultation model

## When to use this skill
Use for policy, legislative, or regulatory initiatives that require structured consultation with public, industry, expert, or institutional stakeholders. Do not use for a generic stakeholder plan unless consultation design is the main deliverable.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as consultation objective, target audiences, methods, governance, and implementation direction.

## Typical output
Typical length: 8–14 pages in a standard version. This is the default for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the consultation deck  
**What it includes:** consultation answer, 3–5 messages, policy implication, execution direction  
**Typical formats:** message page; consultation framework; participation summary

### 1) Consultation objective and policy context — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Why consultation is being run and what decision it supports  
**What it includes:** policy context, objectives, key decision points, consultation boundaries  
**Typical formats:** context page; policy cycle; issue tree

### 2) Audience and participation model — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Who will be consulted and how  
**What it includes:** public, experts, institutions, industry, community groups, participation logic  
**Typical formats:** audience map; participation model; segmentation table

### 3) Methods and channels — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** How consultation will take place  
**What it includes:** surveys, workshops, hearings, digital channels, bilateral outreach, open consultation  
**Typical formats:** channel matrix; method map; consultation journey

### 4) Synthesis and recommendation model — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How inputs will be analyzed and translated  
**What it includes:** feedback clustering, issue synthesis, recommendation rules, escalation of contested points  
**Typical formats:** synthesis framework; heatmap; issue log

### 5) Governance and approvals — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Who oversees and approves the consultation process  
**What it includes:** owners, quality control, escalation, sign-off, transparency logic  
**Typical formats:** governance chart; RACI; approval flow

### 6) Roadmap and engagement calendar — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How consultation is sequenced over time  
**What it includes:** phases, milestones, consultation windows, reporting points  
**Typical formats:** roadmap; calendar; phased plan

### 7) KPI and closure logic — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How consultation quality and completion are tracked  
**What it includes:** participation metrics, issue closure, publication, review cadence  
**Typical formats:** KPI scorecard; dashboard; feedback loop

## How to build
- define the consultation objective and policy decision points it must inform
- segment participant groups and define participation logic
- choose consultation methods, channels, and moments
- design how feedback will be synthesized and fed into policy decisions
- translate into governance, roadmap, and monitoring logic

## Success criteria
The consultation is purposeful, inclusive enough, decision-linked, and governed well enough that input can be turned into credible policy outputs.

## Common failure modes
Consultation for optics only; vague audience logic; methods disconnected from the policy cycle; feedback not synthesized; no governance or closure mechanism.
