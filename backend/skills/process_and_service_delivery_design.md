# Process and service delivery design

## When to use this skill
Use for service redesign, customer journey improvement, back-office process redesign, service delivery reform, or when handoffs and channels are inefficient. Do not use for a narrow organization-only question unless delivery design is in scope.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as service problem, target service model, key process changes, service standards, and implementation direction.

## Typical output
Typical length: 10–16 pages in a standard version. Use this as the default for a broad ask; narrower asks may focus on one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the process and service delivery deck  
**What it includes:** answer, 3–5 messages, service implication, implementation direction  
**Typical formats:** message page; pillar summary; service model snapshot

### 1) Service scope and customer lens — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What services matter and for whom  
**What it includes:** service portfolio, user groups, volume/value criticality, service issues  
**Typical formats:** service map; customer segmentation; portfolio heatmap

### 2) Current-state diagnostic — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** How delivery works today and where it breaks  
**What it includes:** journey pain points, handoffs, channels, delays, controls, rework  
**Typical formats:** journey map; pain-point heatmap; SIPOC

### 3) Service delivery design principles — 1 page
**What it is:** The rules guiding the redesign  
**What it includes:** service priorities, channel principles, efficiency vs control, user-centricity  
**Typical formats:** principles page; design guardrails

### 4) Target service delivery model — 2–4 pages
**What it is:** How services should be delivered in the future  
**What it includes:** service model, channels, ownership, delivery roles, insource vs outsource logic  
**Typical formats:** service blueprint; channel model; interaction model

### 5) Priority process redesign — 2–4 pages
**What it is:** The critical processes that need redesign  
**What it includes:** key process maps, gateways, roles, controls, SLA points  
**Typical formats:** process map; swimlane; RACI

### 6) Service standards and management — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How delivery quality will be managed  
**What it includes:** SLAs, KPIs, customer feedback, case management, escalation  
**Typical formats:** SLA table; KPI scorecard; dashboard mock-up

### 7) Roadmap and implementation — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the redesign will be landed  
**What it includes:** waves, quick wins, automation priorities, governance  
**Typical formats:** roadmap; chevron with sub-steps; phased plan

## How to build
- define the services and customers in scope
- diagnose journey, process, and channel pain points
- set service design principles and target outcomes
- design the target service delivery model and priority processes
- define service standards, ownership, and controls
- translate into roadmap and governance

## Success criteria
Services are clearly defined; process design improves speed and quality; ownership and handoffs are clear; service standards are practical; implementation focuses on the few critical processes.

## Common failure modes
Too process-heavy without service logic; too many detailed maps too early; no ownership model; channels ignored; no standards or improvement priorities.
