# Procurement / supply chain strategy

## When to use this skill
Use when the ask covers sourcing strategy, category strategy, supply chain redesign, supplier model, inventory or logistics strategy, or broader procurement transformation. Typical asks include strategic sourcing, centralization of procurement, network optimization, supplier rationalization, and end-to-end supply chain strategy. Do not use for a generic cost program with no procurement or supply-chain question at its core.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as the value at stake, current pain points, target model, priority categories or supply-chain plays, and implementation direction.

## Typical output
Typical length: 10–16 pages in a standard version. This is the default structure for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the procurement or supply-chain deck  
**What it includes:** value at stake, key issues, target model, value implication, roadmap  
**Typical formats:** message page; procurement strategy summary; recommendation page

### 1) Strategic objective and value at stake — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Why procurement or supply chain matters to the strategy  
**What it includes:** objective, scope, current pain points, expected impact areas  
**Typical formats:** context page; value bridge; issue tree

### 2) Current state baseline — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** How spend, suppliers, categories, and flows work today  
**What it includes:** spend view, supplier landscape, process baseline, service levels, network or inventory baseline  
**Typical formats:** spend cube; value chain map; network map

### 3) Diagnosis and priority issues — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Where the structural issues are  
**What it includes:** category pain points, supplier concentration, fragmentation, service failures, working capital issues  
**Typical formats:** heatmap; Pareto; supplier segmentation

### 4) Target model and strategic choices — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** What the future model should look like  
**What it includes:** category model, sourcing model, make/buy, supplier strategy, network choices, planning logic  
**Typical formats:** target operating model; make-buy matrix; category strategy page

### 5) Priority categories or supply-chain plays — 2–4 pages
**What it is:** What to act on first  
**What it includes:** priority categories, suppliers, nodes, contracts, or inventory plays  
**Typical formats:** portfolio heatmap; category cards; opportunity matrix

### 6) Value, service, and resilience implications — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What the strategy means economically and operationally  
**What it includes:** savings, service outcomes, resilience trade-offs, control implications  
**Typical formats:** trade-off table; savings bridge; risk matrix

### 7) Operating model, governance, and roadmap — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** How the strategy will be delivered  
**What it includes:** governance, process changes, digital enablers, sourcing waves, milestones  
**Typical formats:** governance chart; value-chain roadmap; chevron with sub-steps

## How to build
- clarify the strategic objective across cost, service, resilience, and control
- baseline spend, suppliers, categories, service levels, and value-chain flows
- diagnose structural issues by category or node
- define the target sourcing or supply-chain model
- prioritize categories, suppliers, or network moves
- translate into value, service, and resilience implications
- set the operating model, governance, and roadmap

## Success criteria
The target model is explicit, categories or value-chain priorities are clear, service and resilience trade-offs are visible, and the roadmap is practical.

## Common failure modes
Tactical sourcing ideas with no strategy; no category prioritization; no supplier or network logic; savings divorced from service risk; operating model not addressed.
