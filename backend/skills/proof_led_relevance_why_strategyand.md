# Proof-Led Relevance Narrative (“Why Strategy&”)

## When to use this skill
Use this skill to build the relevance case: why Strategy& is the right partner for this specific client problem, under this specific risk profile. This is not a generic firm overview. The guiding principle is that every “Why Strategy&” point must be proof-led and risk-linked: it should tie back to a client need or delivery risk, and be evidenced with the closest available proof, typically reinforced by dedicated credentials packs.

Do not use this skill as a dumping ground for logos, generic scale claims, or boilerplate firm history.

## Narrative shape

### 1) Client need or delivery risk
**Purpose**  
Start from the client’s real concern: sector complexity, execution risk, institutional sensitivity, pace, ecosystem coordination, local context, specialist expertise, etc.

**Typical length**  
1 framing slide or opening paragraph.

**What must be present**  
The reason the client should care who delivers the work, not just what the work is.

**Common failure modes**  
Opening with “We are a leading firm…” instead of the client’s risk.

### 2) Relevance pillars
**Purpose**  
Show 3–5 reasons we are relevant.

**Typical length**  
3–5 slides or one summary page plus support pages.

**What must be present**  
Each pillar tied to a need or risk; for example, comparable problem solving, sector/ecosystem understanding, ability to execute at scale, local stakeholder familiarity, or specialist tools/capabilities.

**Common failure modes**  
Pillars that are generic and could be reused for any proposal; or too many pillars, diluting the message.

### 3) Proof under each pillar
**Purpose**  
Substantiate the claim.

**Typical length**  
2–4 bullets or exhibits per pillar.

**What must be present**  
Closest credentials by sector, geography, client type, and scope shape; specific tools or assets; or evidence of ecosystem familiarity.

**Common failure modes**  
Logo farms; unrelated “big name” credentials; or copying from old decks without checking relevance.

### 4) Closing implication
**Purpose**  
Translate the proof into a client-facing conclusion: why this lowers risk or increases confidence.

**Typical length**  
1 paragraph or concluding line.

**What must be present**  
A clear statement of why the client should believe Strategy& is the right choice.

**Common failure modes**  
Leaving the proof unconnected to the client decision.

## Page patterns and exhibit types
- **Why us summary page** — 3–5 pillars, each linked to a client need or risk.
- **Pillar page** — one message headline, 2–4 proof points, 1–2 closest credentials.
- **Need-risk-proof map** — client need on the left, our proof on the right.
- **Selected credentials page** — curated examples only, organized by relevance rather than by sector list.

## House conventions
- Build from the client problem outward, not from firm history inward.
- Use the fewest pillars needed to make the case.
- Tie every pillar to a client need or delivery risk.
- Use the closest proof available; if evidence is thin, admit it and avoid overclaiming.
- Keep the tone confident but not generic.
- If using firm-scale statements, explain why scale matters here.

## Inputs the skill needs
**Required**
- Client need or delivery risk.
- Relevant internal proof points.
- Closest supporting credentials.

**Optional**
- External validators.
- Draft team.
- Specialist toolkits.
- Ecosystem or stakeholder map.

## Judgment anchors
- Choose proof by similarity of problem shape first, then by sector, then by geography.
- Use dedicated credentials decks only if they support the current case; do not inherit every message in them.
- If the ask is execution-heavy, lead with delivery proof.
- If the ask is politically or institutionally sensitive, lead with ecosystem and stakeholder familiarity.
- Avoid making the relevance case broader than the actual proposal needs.

## Common failure modes
- Generic adjectives without proof.
- Too many logos and no logic.
- Credentials that show activity, not relevance.
- No explicit link back to client risk.
- Reusing an old Why Strategy& section with light edits.

## Done-state checklist
- Every pillar answers “why us for this client problem?”
- Every pillar has proof.
- Proof is close in shape and context.
- The section would still work even if all logos were removed and only the logic remained.
- The reader can see exactly which risks Strategy& reduces.

## Composition notes
