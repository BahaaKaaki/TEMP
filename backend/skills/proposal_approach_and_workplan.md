# Proposal approach and workplan

## When to use this skill
Use when the ask is specifically for the methodology, workplan, timeline, or delivery logic inside a proposal. This is the right skill when the team already has context and relevance pages and needs the ‘how we will do it’ section. Do not use for a full proposal or for a one-page executive summary only.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full approach and usually contain 3–5 components such as the objective, phases, major outputs, timeline logic, and governance or working model.

## Typical output
Typical length: 4–8 pages in a standard version. This is the default structure for a broad ask; narrower requests may be one page, while more complex scopes may require more detailed phase pages and scope mapping.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 pages
**What it is:** One-page tracker for the approach section  
**What it includes:** objective, phases, outputs, timeline logic, governance highlights  
**Typical formats:** message page; approach summary; chevron overview

### 1) Objective and delivery logic — 1 pages
**What it is:** What the work must achieve and how the plan is structured  
**What it includes:** scope intent, design principles, work logic, success factors  
**Typical formats:** design-principles page; issue-to-phase bridge

### 2) Phase-based methodology — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** The core delivery spine of the project  
**What it includes:** 3–5 phases, phase objectives, entry and exit logic  
**Typical formats:** chevron; staged roadmap; phase ladder

### 3) Activities and deliverables by phase — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What each phase will do and produce  
**What it includes:** key analyses, workshops, outputs, review points  
**Typical formats:** phase matrix; deliverables ladder; workstream map

### 4) Timeline and critical path — 1 pages
**What it is:** When work happens and where the key milestones sit  
**What it includes:** weeks or months, milestone points, overlaps, decision gates  
**Typical formats:** gantt; milestone roadmap; cadence bar

### 5) Governance and ways of working — 1 pages
**What it is:** How the project will run day to day  
**What it includes:** SteerCos, working sessions, PMO rhythm, escalation, QA  
**Typical formats:** governance table; meeting cadence; escalation flow

### 6) Assumptions, dependencies, and scope notes — 1 pages
**What it is:** What the approach depends on to succeed  
**What it includes:** client inputs, access assumptions, exclusions, knowledge transfer logic  
**Typical formats:** assumptions table; dependency tracker; scope map

## How to build
- start from the objective and scope, then shape the phases around the real decision journey
- use 3–5 meaningful phases with clear names and a visible flow
- map activities, deliverables, governance, timeline, and roles to the same phase backbone
- show workshops, decision gates, and key client inputs where they matter
- surface assumptions, dependencies, and exclusions early enough to de-risk the plan
- keep the workplan decision-useful rather than turning it into a task list
- end with mobilization and how the work lands in implementation or approval

## Success criteria
The workplan is easy to follow, the phases are distinct, the client can see what happens when and why, and the same backbone carries activities, deliverables, timeline, workshops, and governance.

## Common failure modes
Too many phases; phases named generically; activities listed without outputs; timeline disconnected from the methodology; no decision gates; assumptions and client dependencies hidden or missing.
