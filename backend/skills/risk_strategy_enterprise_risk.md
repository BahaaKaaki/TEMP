# Risk strategy / enterprise risk

## When to use this skill
Use when the ask is to design or refresh enterprise risk management, risk appetite, KRIs, mitigation approach, or the risk operating model. Typical asks include ERM framework design, group risk strategy, risk governance, portfolio risk, and resilience planning. Do not use for a simple project risk log or a narrow compliance-only review.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as the key risks, appetite or exposure view, mitigation priorities, governance model, and monitoring logic.

## Typical output
Typical length: 10–14 pages in a standard version. This is the default structure for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the risk deck  
**What it includes:** top risks, exposure view, mitigation priorities, governance, implication  
**Typical formats:** message page; risk summary; heatmap page

### 1) Risk mandate and strategic context — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Why risk matters to the organization now  
**What it includes:** risk objective, strategic context, decision-making relevance, scope  
**Typical formats:** context page; risk mandate page; strategy-risk bridge

### 2) Risk universe and taxonomy — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How risks are organized and defined  
**What it includes:** risk categories, definitions, boundaries, enterprise view  
**Typical formats:** risk universe map; taxonomy table; issue tree

### 3) Current exposure and prioritization — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** What the most material risks are  
**What it includes:** risk identification, likelihood and impact, concentration, correlation, prioritization  
**Typical formats:** risk heatmap; bubble chart; concentration view

### 4) Appetite, thresholds, and KRIs — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How much risk is acceptable and how it will be watched  
**What it includes:** risk appetite, trigger levels, KRIs, tolerances  
**Typical formats:** appetite table; KRI dashboard; threshold table

### 5) Mitigation and control priorities — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** What will be done about the top risks  
**What it includes:** mitigation actions, control themes, ownership, residual risk logic  
**Typical formats:** mitigation register; bow-tie; action heatmap

### 6) Governance and operating model — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Who owns risk and how decisions get made  
**What it includes:** roles, committees, CRO or risk-owner model, assurance lines, escalation  
**Typical formats:** governance chart; RACI; committee map

### 7) Monitoring, reporting, and resilience — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How risk management stays alive over time  
**What it includes:** reporting cadence, escalation flow, resilience or continuity actions, next steps  
**Typical formats:** cadence view; escalation flow; dashboard

## How to build
- clarify the strategic context and the role of risk management
- define the risk universe and taxonomy
- assess current exposure and prioritize key risks
- set appetite, thresholds, and KRIs
- define mitigation plans and control logic
- design the governance and operating model
- establish monitoring, escalation, and resilience routines

## Success criteria
Risk is linked to strategy, the top risks are prioritized, appetite and KRIs are actionable, owners are clear, and governance is practical rather than theoretical.

## Common failure modes
Long risk list with no prioritization; no appetite; weak KRIs; no ownership; unclear escalation; mitigation too generic; risk viewed separately from decisions.
