# Sector development strategy and implementation playbook

## When to use this skill
Use this when a ministry, sector custodian, authority, or national program office needs an ecosystem-wide strategy rather than a single-entity plan. Typical trigger phrases include “sector strategy,” “national strategy,” “implementation playbook,” “sector roadmap,” “ecosystem strategy,” and “priority initiatives.” Do **not** use it for an entity’s own corporate strategy, even if that entity operates in the sector; use the corporate strategy skill when the primary question is the client’s own role and performance rather than the full ecosystem.

## Deliverable shape
1. **Executive summary and sector thesis**  
   Purpose: state the sector ambition, the few choices that matter, and what must change systemically.  
   Typical length: 2–4 slides.  
   Complete when: the summary names the strategic thesis, priority themes, sector outcomes, and immediate asks of leadership.  
   Common failure modes: reading like a generic government vision statement, no real choices, no implementation bias.

2. **Case for change and baseline diagnosis**  
   Purpose: quantify why the sector needs intervention.  
   Typical length: 3–5 slides.  
   Complete when: current performance, unmet need, structural bottlenecks, and baseline pain points are visible.  
   Common failure modes: too much descriptive sector context, no quantified problem, weak prioritization of issues.

3. **Benchmarking and lessons learned**  
   Purpose: show how others solved similar problems and what is transferable.  
   Typical length: 2–4 slides.  
   Complete when: benchmark jurisdictions or sectors are chosen for a reason and lessons are translated into local implications.  
   Common failure modes: benchmark tourism-style “beauty parade,” copying models without regard to local mandate, no lessons synthesis.

4. **Sector ecosystem and stakeholder map**  
   Purpose: make visible who shapes outcomes across the value chain.  
   Typical length: 1–3 slides.  
   Complete when: policymakers, regulators, operators, funders, educators, private sector, NGOs, and users are mapped with roles and interaction gaps.  
   Common failure modes: stakeholder list without role clarity, ecosystem map that does not explain where interventions sit.

5. **Strategic positioning, goals, and focus areas**  
   Purpose: articulate the sector’s direction and what it will prioritize.  
   Typical length: 2–3 slides.  
   Complete when: the vision, mission, strategic themes/pillars, and focus areas are explicitly linked to the diagnosis.  
   Common failure modes: generic pillars, too many focus areas, no line of sight to actual bottlenecks.

6. **Enabling pillars**  
   Purpose: define the non-project conditions required for the sector to move.  
   Typical length: 3–5 slides.  
   Complete when: policy/regulation, funding/financing, talent/HCB, data/technology, awareness, and institutional setup are addressed as needed.  
   Common failure modes: strategy reduced to projects only, enablers treated as afterthoughts, unclear role of the sector custodian.

7. **Flagship initiatives and program portfolio**  
   Purpose: translate the strategy into programs and initiatives.  
   Typical length: 3–6 slides.  
   Complete when: initiatives are grouped by pillar, sequenced, and linked to sector outcomes and owners.  
   Common failure modes: no prioritization, no flagship programs, no delivery ownership outside the client.

8. **Governance and delivery model**  
   Purpose: show how the ecosystem will coordinate and who will drive the agenda.  
   Typical length: 2–3 slides.  
   Complete when: forums, committees, PMO/sector delivery unit roles, reporting flows, and escalation logic are clear.  
   Common failure modes: governance built only around the client, no cross-entity coordination, no implementation office logic.

9. **KPI framework and monitoring model**  
   Purpose: define how sector outcomes will be measured.  
   Typical length: 1–2 slides.  
   Complete when: outcome, output, and enabling KPIs are distinguished and assigned to owners or reporting sources.  
   Common failure modes: KPI overload, metrics without data source, only activity KPIs.

10. **Implementation roadmap and quick wins**  
Purpose: phase the strategy into something that can start.  
Typical length: 2–4 slides.  
Complete when: quick wins, foundational reforms, and longer-term structural moves are separated in a practical roadmap.  
Common failure modes: everything starts at once, no sequencing of enablers, no resource or governance implications.

## Visual framework library
- **Baseline — sector challenge heat map**: challenge themes down the side, severity by segment/stakeholder across the top. Use to prioritize what the strategy is solving.
- **Benchmarking — peer lesson cards**: 3–5 benchmark jurisdictions with “what they did / what matters here.” Use instead of lengthy country profiles.
- **Ecosystem — value-chain stakeholder map**: actor groups mapped across the sector journey. Use when responsibility fragmentation is central to the problem.
- **Strategy articulation — sector strategy house**: aspiration at top, 4–6 pillars, 2–3 objectives each, enabling base. Use to keep the strategy tight.
- **Initiives — program portfolio map**: pillars on one axis, time horizon on the other, showing which interventions are structural reforms versus near-term programs.
- **Governance — tiered delivery model**: steering committee, technical committee, working groups, PMO/sector delivery unit. Use when execution spans multiple entities.
- **Monitoring — KPI scorecard**: sector outcomes, enabling metrics, and entity accountability. Use when the client needs a practical monitoring mechanism rather than a dashboard concept.
- **Roadmap — reform waves**: quick wins, foundational reforms, structural changes, capability building. Use when legislation or institution-building must precede downstream initiatives.

## House conventions
- **Action-title format**: titles should state the sector implication, e.g., “The sector’s binding constraints are not demand-side only; governance, capability, and funding failures explain most underperformance.”
- **Executive summary structure**: case for change, strategic thesis, flagship programs, governance model, and first-year priorities.
- **How findings are articulated**: sector fact base → systemic insight → implication for intervention design.
- **Tone**: directive and implementation-minded. Avoid “aspirational policy language” unless it is tied to actual programs and KPIs.
- **Vocabulary**: “sector custodian,” “ecosystem,” “enabling pillars,” “flagship programs,” “delivery unit,” “monitoring framework,” “implementation roadmap,” “policy and institutional enablers.”
- **Boundary discipline**: be explicit about what the sector custodian controls directly versus what it influences through policy, funding, or coordination.

## Inputs the skill needs
- **Sector scope** (**Required**): the sector or policy domain, user groups, and geographic scope.
- **Mandate and sponsor** (**Required**): who owns the strategy and what authority they have.
- **Baseline data** (**Required**): sector performance, participation, service levels, investment, outcome gaps, or proxy indicators.
- **Stakeholder list** (**Required**): entities across the value chain and enabling ecosystem.
- **Existing strategies / initiatives** (**Optional but high value**): to avoid repeating or contradicting existing work.
- **Benchmark direction** (**Optional**): jurisdictions the sponsor wants considered, even if they are later screened out.
- **Policy / legislative constraints** (**Optional but often material**): if reform requires legal change.
- **Political timeline** (**Optional**): approval windows, national-plan dependencies, budget cycles, or public announcements.

## Methodology anchors
- Sector strategy is different from entity strategy: always separate ecosystem interventions from the sponsor’s own operating agenda.
- Benchmark structures and reform programs, not just end-state policy statements.
- Show who must act beyond the sponsor; many sector strategies fail because they read like single-agency plans.
- Balance flagship visible programs with foundational enabling reforms; both matter, but they should be sequenced differently.
- Keep the KPI framework practical and data-aware; sector monitoring often fails on data availability more than on metric design.
- Make governance real: define a delivery unit, committee structure, and reporting rhythm if implementation spans multiple entities.

## Done-state checklist
- The strategy clearly answers what systemic problem is being solved and why the sponsor is the right convener.
- The ecosystem map is useful, not decorative.
- Benchmarks change the design rather than merely validate the intuition.
- Strategic pillars are distinct and traceable to the diagnosis.
- The initiative portfolio names owners beyond the client where needed.
- Enabling reforms are explicit and phased.
- Governance and PMO / delivery-unit design are practical.
- KPIs include outcomes, not just activities.
- Quick wins exist, but they do not substitute for structural reform.
- The pack can withstand the challenge: “Who exactly needs to do what, by when?”

## Composition notes
This skill often composes with **Regulatory / legislative reform strategy and implementation plan**, **Kick-off, workplan and data request pack**, and **Weekly SteerCo / PMO status deck**. Use the regulatory skill when the reform of rules and institutions is itself the main deliverable rather than just one enabling pillar.
