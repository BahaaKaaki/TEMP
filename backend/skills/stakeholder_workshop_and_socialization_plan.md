# Stakeholder Workshop and Socialization Plan

## When to use this skill
Use this skill when the client needs not just a stakeholder map, but a deliberate sequence of workshops, review forums, and socialization moments to unlock decisions, alignment, and buy-in. This is the right skill when progress depends on co-creation sessions, steering checkpoints, targeted 1:1 socialization, and a clear path from working-level debate to senior approval.

Treat workshops as decision infrastructure rather than meeting admin: major workshops as the backbone of strategy approval, co-creation labs with phase-based messaging and approval paths, and pilot workshops embedded in the target-state interaction model. Use this skill when the artifact must specify which sessions happen, why they happen, what they decide, and how outcomes are socialized.

Do not use it for a generic meeting calendar. If the objective is adoption and behavior change, invoke **Change Management and Communication Plan**. If the objective is broader stakeholder orchestration across the initiative lifecycle, invoke **Stakeholder Engagement Plan**.

## Deliverable shape

### 1) Decision map and audience architecture
**Purpose**  
Clarify which decisions or alignments must be created and which audiences matter for each.

**Typical length**  
1 slide.

**What must be present**  
Decision topics; stakeholders required in the room; and which audiences need co-creation, validation, or only socialization.

**Common failure modes**  
Starting with workshop logistics; inviting everyone to everything; or treating all sessions as “alignment” rather than distinguishing design from approval.

### 2) Forum architecture
**Purpose**  
Define the types of sessions to be used across the program.

**Typical length**  
1–2 slides.

**What must be present**  
A small set of forum types such as working sessions, decision workshops, SteerCos, pre-board socialization, technical deep dives, and targeted 1:1s.

**Common failure modes**  
Too many forum types; no difference between workshops and governance bodies; or forum names that do not indicate what gets decided.

### 3) Session objectives and outputs
**Purpose**  
Specify what each major workshop or socialization step is meant to produce.

**Typical length**  
2–4 slides.

**What must be present**  
For each session: objective, pre-read, participants, facilitation method, expected outputs, and what happens next.

**Common failure modes**  
Agendas with no decision output; sessions framed as presentations rather than working forums; or no clear handoff into the next phase.

### 4) Facilitation and interaction design
**Purpose**  
Show how sessions will create useful input and decisions.

**Typical length**  
1–3 slides.

**What must be present**  
Mechanics such as co-creation, working groups, prioritization exercises, challenge sessions, decision validation, and capture of dissenting views.

**Common failure modes**  
Over-indexing on presentations; not separating exploration from decision; or no structure for surfacing blockers.

### 5) Socialization path before and after formal decisions
**Purpose**  
Design the route from draft content to formal endorsement.

**Typical length**  
1–2 slides.

**What must be present**  
Pre-socialization with critical stakeholders; formal review path; targeted bilateral sessions for likely blockers; and post-workshop follow-up or rework logic.

**Common failure modes**  
Assuming a steering committee is the first real review; forcing unresolved issues into formal governance; or no plan for dissent management.

### 6) Timeline and cadence
**Purpose**  
Map workshops and socialization moments to the broader program timeline.

**Typical length**  
1–2 slides.

**What must be present**  
Decision gates, work products leading into each session, and how sessions line up with program phases.

**Common failure modes**  
A calendar disconnected from the workplan; too many workshops clustered with no absorption time; or no distinction between milestone workshops and routine check-ins.

### 7) Output capture and decision log
**Purpose**  
Ensure workshops become management tools, not conversations that fade.

**Typical length**  
1 slide.

**What must be present**  
Minutes or summary format, decision log, open points, owners, target dates, and revision loop.

**Common failure modes**  
No record of what was agreed; no owner for unresolved items; or no clear decision trail for leadership.

### 8) Governance link and escalation path
**Purpose**  
Connect workshop outputs to the formal governance structure.

**Typical length**  
1 slide.

**What must be present**  
Which outputs feed SteerCos, boards, approval bodies, or program milestones; and how unresolved matters get escalated.

**Common failure modes**  
Workshops running in parallel to governance with no bridge; or over-escalating issues that should be resolved in working sessions.

## Page patterns and exhibit types
Use a small set of repeatable pages.

- **Decision ladder** — topic, working session, validation session, formal approval point.
- **Forum architecture map** — workshop types, purpose, participants, and outputs.
- **Milestone workshop calendar** — major workshops and review sessions linked to phases.
- **Session design page** — objective, agenda blocks, output, pre-read, owner.
- **Socialization funnel** — broad co-creation, targeted socialization, formal endorsement.
- **Decision and open-points tracker** — what was decided, what remains open, owner, due date.

The best workshop plans feel like control systems, not calendars.

## House conventions
- Define the decision before defining the meeting.
- Use workshops as decision gates, not as show-and-tell forums.
- Separate co-creation, challenge, validation, and approval.
- Pre-socialize likely blockers before formal governance wherever possible.
- Keep participant groups tight; broader audiences usually belong in socialization, not design.
- Every major session ends with outputs, owners, and next steps.
- Build explicit loops between workshop outputs and governance papers.
- Use targeted 1:1s or small-group reviews for politically sensitive issues; do not force everything into the big room.

## Inputs the skill needs
**Required**
- Program phases and major deliverables.
- Key decisions or alignment topics to be unlocked.
- Stakeholder groups and likely approvers.
- Existing governance bodies and approval path.
- Known sensitivities or likely blockers.

**Optional**
- Existing meeting calendar.
- Board pack or SteerCo templates.
- Availability constraints and critical dates.
- Preferred facilitation style or room format.
- Prior workshop readouts.

The skill should degrade gracefully if the agenda details are immature; it should not degrade gracefully if the decision path is unknown.

## Judgment anchors
- Decide which issues need workshop-level co-creation and which need bilateral socialization.
- Keep the number of milestone workshops small and consequential.
- Use formal governance only for matters that are mature enough to decide.
- Match the format to the moment: whiteboarding for exploration, structured choice for prioritization, crisp pre-read plus questions for validation.
- Socialization is a designed activity, not a courtesy; use it to remove surprises from approval forums.
- Make output capture hard-edged. If there is no decision or no next step, the session probably should not happen.

## Common failure modes
- Workshops are scheduled before the core thinking is ready.
- Too many people are invited, so nothing gets decided.
- Sessions are presentation-led rather than work-led.
- There is no pre-read discipline.
- The same debate repeats across multiple forums because outputs are not captured cleanly.
- Formal approvals fail because critical stakeholders were never pre-socialized.

## Done-state checklist
- Every workshop has one job and a visible output.
- The forum architecture is simple enough to run and strong enough to control the program.
- The socialization path removes avoidable surprises from formal governance.
- Decisions, open points, and owners are documented.
- The cadence aligns with the program phases.
- The client can see which sessions are essential and which are routine.
- The plan anticipates blocker management, not just ideal alignment.

## Composition notes
This skill usually composes with:
- **Stakeholder Engagement Plan**
- **Governance Cadence and Decision Forum Design**
- **Change Management and Communication Plan**

Use this skill when the hard part is alignment and decision creation. If the hard part is sustained adoption after the decision, the change skill should lead.

## Metadata
- Primary use: strategy design, operating model refresh, transformation mobilization, policy design
- Typical consumer seniority: manager to director
- Typical output length: 4–10 slides
- Durability: high
- Suggested owner discipline: strategy office / PMO / transformation office
