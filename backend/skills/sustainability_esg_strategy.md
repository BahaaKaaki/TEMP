# Sustainability / ESG strategy

## When to use this skill
Use for ESG strategy, sustainability roadmap, net zero or environmental agenda, corporate sustainability transformation, or public-sector sustainability direction. Do not use for a pure disclosure exercise or a detailed reporting manual.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as baseline and materiality, ambition, priority pillars, operating implications, and implementation direction.

## Typical output
Typical length: 10–16 pages in a standard version. This is the default for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the ESG deck  
**What it includes:** ESG answer, 3–5 messages, impact implication, execution direction  
**Typical formats:** message page; pillar summary; ESG strategy house

### 1) Sustainability context and baseline — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** Current performance and why the agenda matters  
**What it includes:** baseline, material issues, key risks and opportunities, strategic context  
**Typical formats:** baseline snapshot; materiality view; issue tree

### 2) Benchmark and standards perspective — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** External requirements and leading practices  
**What it includes:** standards, peer positioning, benchmark practices, market expectations  
**Typical formats:** benchmark table; peer comparison; standards map

### 3) Ambition and strategic pillars — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** What the sustainability strategy is trying to achieve  
**What it includes:** ambition, objectives, 3–5 pillars, target outcomes  
**Typical formats:** pillar page; strategy house; target-state view

### 4) Priority initiatives — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** What needs to happen to deliver the strategy  
**What it includes:** initiatives, no-regret moves, value logic, prioritization  
**Typical formats:** initiative tree; prioritization matrix; heatmap

### 5) Operating model, data, and reporting implications — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** What must change in how the organization works  
**What it includes:** governance, org ownership, data, systems, reporting, controls  
**Typical formats:** operating model page; governance chart; capability heatmap

### 6) Roadmap and implementation — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the strategy will be delivered  
**What it includes:** waves, milestones, responsibilities, key enablers  
**Typical formats:** roadmap; phased plan; chevron with sub-steps

### 7) KPIs and governance — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How performance will be monitored  
**What it includes:** KPI set, targets, governance, review cadence  
**Typical formats:** KPI scorecard; governance chart; cadence view

## How to build
- define the sustainability question and business or policy rationale
- assess baseline performance, material topics, and current gaps
- benchmark standards, peers, and leading practices
- define ambition, objectives, and strategic pillars
- translate into initiatives, data / reporting needs, and operating implications
- close with roadmap, KPIs, and governance

## Typical data needed
current ESG / sustainability performance; materiality inputs; external standards and regulatory expectations; peer practices; emissions / resource / social data; organizational ownership; current initiatives and systems

## Success criteria
Strategy is material and focused; ambition is clear; pillars are linked to business or policy value; operating and data implications are explicit; KPIs and governance are clear.

## Common failure modes
ESG wish list; no materiality logic; no business linkage; no data and reporting implications; weak ownership and roadmap.
