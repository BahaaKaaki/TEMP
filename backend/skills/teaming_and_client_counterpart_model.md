# Teaming and client counterpart model

## When to use this skill
Use when success depends on a clear joint working model between the consulting team and the client, especially in complex, multi-workstream, or multi-stakeholder engagements. Do not use for a generic org chart unless the joint delivery model is the point.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as delivery model, role split, governance, meeting rhythm, and implementation direction.

## Typical output
Typical length: 6–10 pages in a standard version. This is the default for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the teaming deck  
**What it includes:** teaming answer, 3–5 messages, delivery implication, execution direction  
**Typical formats:** message page; teaming framework; model summary

### 1) Engagement objective and delivery principles — 1 page
**What it is:** Why the joint model exists and how it should work  
**What it includes:** engagement objectives, delivery principles, success definition  
**Typical formats:** context page; guiding principles; strategy house

### 2) Teaming structure and counterparts — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Who is involved and how the teams line up  
**What it includes:** consulting team, client counterparts, workstream leads, sponsor roles  
**Typical formats:** team structure; org chart; role table

### 3) Workstream ownership and deliverable split — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Who owns what in practice  
**What it includes:** roles, responsibilities, workstream ownership, key handoffs  
**Typical formats:** RACI; ownership matrix; workstream map

### 4) Governance and decision rhythm — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How meetings and decisions will work  
**What it includes:** SteerCos, PMO, workstream meetings, approvals, escalation  
**Typical formats:** governance chart; cadence view; meeting architecture

### 5) Day-to-day operating model — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How work gets done between meetings  
**What it includes:** collaboration rules, trackers, issue management, draft / review flow  
**Typical formats:** operating model page; tracker view; process map

### 6) Knowledge transfer and capability build — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the client team will absorb and retain the work  
**What it includes:** shadowing, co-creation, handover, training, documentation  
**Typical formats:** knowledge-transfer model; ladder; roadmap

### 7) Roadmap and handoff logic — 1 page
**What it is:** How the teaming model evolves through the engagement  
**What it includes:** phases, transition points, closeout and handover moments  
**Typical formats:** roadmap; phased plan; handoff plan

## How to build
- define the engagement objectives and delivery principles
- clarify the workstream and counterpart structure
- set governance, meetings, and decision rhythm
- define role split, deliverable ownership, and escalation logic
- translate into trackers, handoffs, and knowledge-transfer model

## Success criteria
The team model is clear, ownership is unambiguous, governance supports delivery speed, and the client knows how to work with the team day to day.

## Common failure modes
Team chart with no role split; unclear ownership of outputs; too many meetings; weak escalation; no knowledge-transfer or handoff logic.
