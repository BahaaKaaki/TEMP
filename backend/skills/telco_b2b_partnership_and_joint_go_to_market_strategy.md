# Telco B2B partnership and joint go-to-market strategy

## When to use this skill
Use this when a telecom, ICT, or digital infrastructure player is expanding into new B2B solution spaces through a partner-led or alliance-led model and needs a structured GTM, vertical, and operating answer. Typical trigger phrases include “joint GTM,” “partnership model,” “solution play,” “vertical solutions,” “new frontiers,” and “product activation.” Do **not** use it for a pure internal product strategy with no partner dependency, or for a full corporate strategy refresh.

## Deliverable shape
1. **Executive summary and partnership thesis**  
   Purpose: state why the partnership exists, where it will play first, and how it creates value.  
   Typical length: 2–3 slides.  
   Complete when: the vertical focus, use-case thesis, partnership model, and rollout approach are explicit.  
   Common failure modes: tech hype summary, no commercial answer, no priority verticals.

2. **Strategic rationale and aspiration alignment**  
   Purpose: explain why the telco should enter this space and what role the partner plays.  
   Typical length: 1–2 slides.  
   Complete when: the aspiration, growth logic, localization or hosting rationale, and key strategic constraints are clear.  
   Common failure modes: vague adjacency rationale, no reason for partnership over build or buy.

3. **Market and vertical attractiveness assessment**  
   Purpose: identify where demand is real and worth pursuing.  
   Typical length: 2–4 slides.  
   Complete when: priority verticals or customer segments are screened using market size, growth, relevance, and client fit.  
   Common failure modes: too many verticals, weak prioritization, no distinction between attractive and addressable.

4. **Partner capability benchmark and model selection**  
   Purpose: decide how deep the partnership should be.  
   Typical length: 2–3 slides.  
   Complete when: strategic collaboration, commercial agreement, JV, program track, or co-development options are compared with clear trade-offs.  
   Common failure modes: defaulting to a JV, no speed-to-market lens, no capability-build implications.

5. **Use-case identification and prioritization**  
   Purpose: translate verticals into sellable solution plays.  
   Typical length: 3–5 slides.  
   Complete when: the longlist is screened on demand and capability fit, and a priority set is selected.  
   Common failure modes: use-case laundry list, no scoring logic, no tie-back to partner offering strengths.

6. **Joint offering and value proposition**  
   Purpose: define what is actually sold and why the combined offer wins.  
   Typical length: 2–3 slides.  
   Complete when: core offer components, localization / data residency angle, target customer pain points, and differentiated value are clear.  
   Common failure modes: product catalog instead of a proposition, no customer problem, no combined-solution logic.

7. **Commercial model and GTM architecture**  
   Purpose: show how the offer reaches the market and monetizes.  
   Typical length: 3–5 slides.  
   Complete when: target segments, channel model, pricing / licensing / subscription logic, sales motions, and after-sales model are defined.  
   Common failure modes: no segment prioritization, no commercial model, channels not mapped to customer types.

8. **Roles, responsibilities, and operating model**  
   Purpose: define who owns product, sales, delivery, support, and escalation.  
   Typical length: 2–3 slides.  
   Complete when: product ownership, enablement, delivery, L1/L2/L3 support, and governance forums are explicit.  
   Common failure modes: joint ownership of everything, unclear support boundary, no local capability-build plan.

9. **Capability build and phased rollout**  
   Purpose: show how the telco becomes credible over time.  
   Typical length: 1–3 slides.  
   Complete when: training, certification, delivery capability, commercial collateral, and pilot-to-scale sequencing are stated.  
   Common failure modes: assuming the organization is already ready, no enablement path, no pilot logic.

10. **Implementation roadmap and KPI framework**  
Purpose: move the partnership from concept to launch.  
Typical length: 2–3 slides.  
Complete when: the roadmap covers enablement, pilot launch, scaling, governance, and success metrics.  
Common failure modes: no KPI logic, no sequencing, no partner governance.

## Visual framework library
- **Market assessment — vertical attractiveness heat map**: verticals on rows, criteria on columns (market size, growth, strategic fit, partner strength, localization relevance). Use to narrow focus.
- **Partnership model — commitment spectrum**: commercial agreement → strategic collaboration → JV / equity route. Use when leadership is gravitating to heavy structures too early.
- **Use-case screening — weighted scorecard**: demand potential × offering fit. Use for longlists of smart-city, education, manufacturing, government, or sector-specific plays.
- **Joint offer — solution stack map**: partner products / platforms, telco infra / cloud / integration / managed services, customer-facing bundle. Use to show why the combined offer is more than resale.
- **GTM — segment-channel matrix**: enterprise, government, SME, mega-project, key account mapped against direct, virtual, indirect, marketplace, and ecosystem channels.
- **Commercial model — revenue ladder**: license, subscription, implementation fees, managed services, usage-based cloud, commission / co-created packages.
- **Support model — L1/L2/L3 swimlane**: telco handles customer-facing L1/L2, partner owns deep L3, with escalation SLAs.
- **Roadmap — pilot-to-scale roadmap**: enablement, collateral, first customers, case study creation, expansion to new verticals.

## House conventions
- **Action-title format**: titles should read like commercial decisions, e.g., “A staged strategic collaboration focused on two verticals creates faster revenue and lower integration risk than a JV-led launch.”
- **Executive summary structure**: why this play, where to start, how the partnership works, and what is needed to launch.
- **How findings are articulated**: market signal → use-case relevance → combined-solution implication → GTM consequence.
- **Tone**: commercially specific and tech-literate, but not vendor-brochure language.
- **Vocabulary**: “joint offering,” “vertical play,” “use case,” “data residency,” “PartnerEdge / partner track,” “L1/L2/L3 support,” “pilot verticals,” “co-development,” “managed services.”
- **Sales orientation**: the deck should always answer “Who buys?”, “Why now?”, and “How do we win?”.

## Inputs the skill needs
- **Client aspiration** (**Required**): why the telco is entering the space and what outcomes matter.
- **Partner profile and capabilities** (**Required**): product strengths, deployment model, geographic presence, support model.
- **Market and vertical data** (**Required**): size, growth, buyer behavior, solution demand, RFP / localization context.
- **Internal capabilities** (**Required**): sales force, integration capability, cloud / data center footprint, support model, brand strength.
- **Potential use-case longlist** (**Optional but high value**): especially if prior ideation exists.
- **Commercial constraints** (**Optional**): exclusivity, white-label rules, hosting obligations, margin expectations.
- **Rollout constraints** (**Optional**): certification time, localization requirements, procurement cycle, existing JV constraints.

## Methodology anchors
- Start from vertical and use-case attractiveness, not from the partner’s product catalogue.
- Prefer lighter partnership models early unless exclusivity or deep co-development materially changes the economics.
- Make localization / local hosting / data protection explicit in KSA and GCC contexts; these often change the value proposition materially.
- Define roles and escalation precisely; vague joint ownership usually kills GTM velocity.
- Pilot on 1–2 verticals with clear references before broadening the play.
- Show the telco’s “right to win” beyond distribution—local hosting, integration, managed service, compliance, and customer access are common proof points.

## Done-state checklist
- Priority verticals and use cases are clear.
- The partnership model is chosen for a reason, not by habit.
- The joint offering is understandable to a sales leader, not just a product manager.
- Target segments and channel logic are explicit.
- Support roles and escalation paths are defined.
- Capability build is realistic and phased.
- The roadmap starts with a narrow, credible wedge.
- KPIs cover pipeline, conversion, delivery readiness, and support quality.
- The deck can answer “Why partner?” and “Why these verticals first?”
- The commercial model is visible and not hidden in appendices.

## Composition notes
This skill often composes with **Corporate strategy – full strategic plan**, **Target operating model – design and activation blueprint**, and **Final client readout / board decision deck**. If the core question is whether to partner, acquire, or build outright, pair it with **Board-ready way-forward / transaction option recommendation**.

- **Primary sectors:** telecom, ICT, digital infrastructure

- **Typical team size:** 3–6 consultants plus sector / technology SMEs  
- **Primary consumer seniority:** manager / partner  
- **Decay risk:** medium to high  
- **Suggested owner discipline:** telecom and growth strategy
