# Value proposition and offering design

## When to use this skill
Use for new offering design, proposition refresh, portfolio redesign, service concepting, and productization. Do not use for a broad corporate strategy or standalone pricing exercise.

## Executive summary
One page only. It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 messages around target customer, unmet need, proposition choice, offer structure, and commercial implication.

## Typical output
Typical length: 6–10 pages in a standard version. This is the default structure for a broad ask; narrower requests may cover only one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page proposition storyline tracker  
**What it includes:** answer, 3–5 messages, implication for the offer  
**Typical formats:** message page, proposition summary, pillar page

### 1) Customer need and problem — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What problem the offer must solve  
**What it includes:** target users, needs, pain points, moments of truth  
**Typical formats:** need-state map, pain-point summary, customer lens

### 2) Target segment and use cases — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Where the offer should focus  
**What it includes:** priority segments, use cases, buying context, importance  
**Typical formats:** segment matrix, use-case map, prioritization

### 3) Current offer and gaps — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What exists today and what is missing  
**What it includes:** current portfolio, competitive offer set, gaps, overlap  
**Typical formats:** gap analysis, benchmark table, offer map

### 4) Target value proposition — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** The core promise of the offer  
**What it includes:** customer benefit, differentiators, proof points, reasons to choose  
**Typical formats:** proposition stack, feature-benefit table, message pyramid

### 5) Offering architecture and packaging — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the offer is structured  
**What it includes:** modules, bundles, tiers, service components, packaging logic  
**Typical formats:** offer architecture, package ladder, bundle map

### 6) Commercial and launch implications — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What the proposition means commercially  
**What it includes:** pricing direction, channel implications, launch priorities, enablement  
**Typical formats:** implication page, launch roadmap, prioritization

## How to build
- define the target need clearly
- prioritize segments and use cases
- assess current and competitor offers
- articulate the winning proposition
- structure the offer into coherent packages or modules
- translate the proposition into launch implications

## Success criteria
- proposition is clear and differentiated
- customer need is explicit
- offer structure is coherent
- commercial implications are visible
- deck can guide design and launch decisions

## Common failure modes
- proposition too generic
- too many segments treated equally
- offer architecture unclear
- features listed without customer logic
- no implication for commercialization
