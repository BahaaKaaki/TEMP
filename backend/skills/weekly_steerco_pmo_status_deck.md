# Weekly SteerCo / PMO status deck

## When to use this skill

Use this once delivery is underway and senior stakeholders need a disciplined weekly or bi-weekly view of progress, decisions, risks, and actions. Typical trigger phrases include “weekly status,” “SteerCo pack,” “PMO deck,” “RAID report,” and “program dashboard.” Do **not** use it as the project kick-off pack or the final answer deck; it is a running governance deliverable, not a one-off readout.

## Deliverable shape

1. **Executive dashboard**
  Purpose: give leadership a one-page read on overall status.  
   Typical length: 1 slide.  
   Complete when: overall RAG, major wins, slippages, and decisions needed are visible.  
   Common failure modes: too much detail, no leadership ask, inconsistent metrics week to week.
2. **Progress against plan**
  Purpose: show delivery versus agreed milestones.  
   Typical length: 1–2 slides.  
   Complete when: milestones, completed items, next-period deliverables, and any slippage are explicit.  
   Common failure modes: activity list instead of plan view, no variance explanation.
3. **Workstream updates**
  Purpose: summarize what each workstream has done and what it needs.  
   Typical length: 1–3 slides depending on program size.  
   Complete when: key progress, blockers, and next-step actions are shown per workstream.  
   Common failure modes: verbose narratives, inconsistent format across workstreams.
4. **Decisions and escalations required**
  Purpose: force leadership attention onto what only they can solve.  
   Typical length: 1 slide.  
   Complete when: each decision request names the issue, recommendation, consequence of no decision, owner, and due date.  
   Common failure modes: hidden asks buried in workstream pages, no due dates, unclear owner.
5. **RAID log (top only)**
  Purpose: track what threatens delivery.  
   Typical length: 1–2 slides.  
   Complete when: the top risks, issues, assumptions, and dependencies are prioritized with mitigations and owners.  
   Common failure modes: full RAID dump in main pack, no prioritization, stale items.
6. **Resource and stakeholder actions**
  Purpose: show whether access, staffing, or engagement is becoming the bottleneck.  
   Typical length: 1 slide.  
   Complete when: resource constraints, stakeholder asks, and support needed from the client are visible.  
   Common failure modes: pretending the program is on track while access or staffing is failing.
7. **Appendix trackers**
  Purpose: keep detail available without cluttering the main story.  
   Typical length: backup only.  
   Complete when: detailed logs, workplans, issue lists, and KPI tables are available behind the summary.  
   Common failure modes: no appendix support, or the reverse—everything pushed to appendix with no synthesis.

## Visual framework library

- **Executive dashboard — one-page RAG summary**: status by delivery, schedule, quality, stakeholder, and budget/resource where relevant.
- **Milestone tracker — plan vs actual view**: use to show slippage clearly rather than narrating it.
- **Decision log — compact table**: issue, decision required, recommendation, consequence, owner, date.
- **RAID — top 10 only**: keep the main pack focused; full RAID lives in appendix or PMO tool.
- **Benefit tracker — realized vs committed**: use for transformation and cost programs where benefits matter more than deliverables.
- **Heat maps — stakeholder / workstream heat**: useful when some workstreams are green but stakeholder alignment is red.
- **Appendix — detailed PMO registers**: risks, issues, milestone log, resource tracker, and action log.

## House conventions

- **Action-title format**: every page should tell leadership what changed or what matters this week.
- **Executive summary structure**: current status, what changed since last session, what needs decision.
- **How findings are articulated**: status → implication → leadership ask.
- **Tone**: crisp and unsentimental. A SteerCo pack should not read like internal notes.
- **Vocabulary**: “RAG,” “RAID,” “milestone,” “decision required,” “owner,” “mitigation,” “playback,” “next two weeks.”
- **Consistency**: preserve the same dashboard logic every cycle so trend is visible.

## Inputs the skill needs

- **Latest workplan and milestones** (**Required**)
- **Workstream owner updates** (**Required**)
- **RAID register** (**Required**)
- **Decision log / pending asks** (**Required**)
- **Benefit tracker or KPI dashboard** (**Optional but high value**)
- **Resource / staffing status** (**Optional**)
- **Client stakeholder issues** (**Optional**)

## Methodology anchors

- Show only the top issues; the deck is for decisions, not archiving.
- Keep the same structure every cycle so leadership can compare week over week.
- Put decisions on their own page; never hide them inside status text.
- Distinguish schedule slippage from content / quality risk.
- Use the appendix for evidence and details; keep the main pack crisp.

## Done-state checklist

- A senior sponsor can tell in 30 seconds whether the program is on track.
- Decision asks are explicit and actionable.
- Workstream updates are consistent and concise.
- RAID items are current and owned.
- Slippages have implications and recovery actions.
- The pack is comparable to the previous cycle.
- There is no false green masking a real red risk.

## Composition notes

This skill typically follows **Kick-off, workplan, and data request pack** and precedes **Final client readout / board decision deck**. It also composes well with cost-transformation, PMO-heavy, and multi-workstream public-sector programs.

## Metadata

- **Primary sectors:** cross-sector  
- **Typical engagement duration:** recurring through program life  
- **Typical team size:** PMO plus all workstream leads  
- **Primary consumer seniority:** manager / partner / sponsor  
- **Decay risk:** low  
- **Suggested owner discipline:** PMO and program governance
