# Why Strategy&

## When to use this skill
Use when the ask is a Why Strategy&, relevance case, value proposition page, or credentials section inside a proposal. It is the right skill when the client already understands the problem and now needs confidence that we are the right advisor. Do not use for a generic credentials deck or for a full proposal storyline on its own.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the Why Strategy& case and usually contain 3–5 components such as the client need, relevance pillars, proof, risk reduction, and delivery confidence.

## Typical output
Typical length: 2–5 pages in a standard version. This is the default structure for a broad ask; narrower asks may be one page, while complex tenders may require deeper proof and multiple case tiles.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 pages
**What it is:** One-page tracker for the relevance case  
**What it includes:** client need, relevance pillars, proof, delivery confidence, implication  
**Typical formats:** message page; need-proof summary

### 1) Client need and risk lens — 1 pages
**What it is:** What the client needs and what would make delivery difficult  
**What it includes:** need statement, success factors, delivery risks, evaluation lens  
**Typical formats:** need-risk matrix; challenge page

### 2) Relevance pillars — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** The core reasons we should win  
**What it includes:** 3–5 pillars, each tied to need and risk  
**Typical formats:** pillar cards; strategy house; proof pillars

### 3) Proof and case evidence — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Comparable proof points that substantiate the pillars  
**What it includes:** case tiles, credential snippets, ecosystem knowledge, analogous work  
**Typical formats:** case study tiles; evidence table; credential matrix

### 4) Platform and delivery confidence — 1 pages
**What it is:** Why we can deliver at the required scale and depth  
**What it includes:** strategy-to-execution logic, SME bench, local context, integration capability  
**Typical formats:** capability stack; delivery model page

### 5) Close and confidence to deliver — 1 pages
**What it is:** A concise closing that lands the relevance case  
**What it includes:** why now, why us, expected client confidence  
**Typical formats:** message page; recommendation close

## How to build
- start from the client need and delivery risk, not from our credentials inventory
- select only 3–5 relevance pillars that are directly supportable
- tie every pillar to what it enables for the client and what risk it reduces
- use proof that is genuinely comparable in scope, geography, sector, or delivery pattern
- show Strategy& plus broader platform value only where it strengthens the case
- keep claims specific and avoid adjectives that are not earned by proof
- make the final page feel like confidence to deliver, not a logo collage

## Success criteria
Each pillar is relevant, specific, and evidenced; the section shows why we are credible for this client and scope; and the client can see the link between our proof and their delivery risk.

## Common failure modes
Generic claims; logos without explanation; proof that is not comparable; no client linkage; no risk logic; saying ‘global network’ or ‘deep expertise’ without showing what that means here.
