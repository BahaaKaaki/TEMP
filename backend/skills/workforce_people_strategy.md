# Workforce / people strategy

## When to use this skill
Use for workforce transformation, capability agenda, talent readiness, future-of-work questions, nationalization or localization agenda, or major strategy execution readiness. Do not use for a narrow HR-process fix unless the broader workforce question matters.

## Executive summary
**One page only.**  
It should track the full deck and usually contain 3–5 components such as workforce challenge, future workforce choices, people priorities, capability implications, and implementation direction.

## Typical output
Typical length: 10–16 pages in a standard version. Use this as the default for a broad ask; narrower asks may focus on one section or page, while deeper asks may extend the deck.

### 0) Executive summary — 1 page
**What it is:** One-page storyline tracker for the workforce deck  
**What it includes:** answer, 3–5 messages, workforce implication, implementation direction  
**Typical formats:** message page; pillar summary; workforce snapshot

### 1) Workforce context and challenge — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** Why the workforce agenda matters now  
**What it includes:** strategy context, workforce pressures, talent risks, key people issues  
**Typical formats:** context page; issue tree; workforce heatmap

### 2) Current workforce and capability gaps — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** How the workforce looks today and where gaps sit  
**What it includes:** workforce shape, critical roles, capability gaps, deployment issues, cultural constraints  
**Typical formats:** heatmap; segmentation view; capability matrix

### 3) Future workforce choices — 1–3 pages
**What it is:** What workforce the strategy requires  
**What it includes:** future skills, workforce size and mix logic, buy / build / borrow choices, critical roles  
**Typical formats:** 2x2; workforce pyramid; capability model

### 4) People strategy pillars — 2–4 pages
**What it is:** How the workforce challenge will be addressed  
**What it includes:** attraction, development, deployment, leadership, rewards, culture, ways of working  
**Typical formats:** pillar page; initiative tree; priority matrix

### 5) Enablement and change — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** What must change in management practice  
**What it includes:** operating implications, change enablers, leadership role, communications, policy implications  
**Typical formats:** change map; stakeholder map; capability heatmap

### 6) Roadmap and KPIs — 1–2 pages
**What it is:** How the people strategy will be tracked and delivered  
**What it includes:** waves, milestones, KPI set, accountability, review cadence  
**Typical formats:** roadmap; KPI scorecard; cadence view

## How to build
- clarify the business or policy agenda the workforce must support
- diagnose current workforce shape, capability gaps, and risks
- define future workforce demand and required capabilities
- set the people strategy pillars and interventions
- translate into roadmap, owners, and KPIs

## Success criteria
Workforce choices are linked to strategy; future capabilities are explicit; interventions are practical; ownership is clear; people agenda is not just HR activity.

## Common failure modes
Generic HR list; no link to strategic priorities; capability gaps are vague; no sequencing; culture and change are ignored.
